# Team leadership in virtual and hybrid work

**Dissertation:** *The relationship between effective team leadership and evolving work models in a small financial services company: A study of Company X*  
**Author:** Claudio Mares (2021)  
**Best for:** A current, well-structured qualitative study of office-based, virtual, and hybrid team leadership.

## Research question

> What makes for effective team leadership during the evolution from office-based to virtual to hybrid working in a case study organisation?

## Objectives

The dissertation aims to:

1. Review team leadership in general and in virtual and hybrid environments.
2. Gather leader and follower perspectives on how teams are led and whether leadership differs by work mode.
3. Identify effective team leadership practices and compare them with published theory.
4. Recommend ways to strengthen positive leadership across virtual and hybrid work, with emphasis on the long-term hybrid model.

## How the dissertation was built

### Research design

This is an exploratory, cross-sectional case study of “Company X,” a 15-person London financial-services firm that moved rapidly from office work to fully virtual work and then to hybrid work during COVID-19.

- Ten employees—66% of the organisation—participated in semi-structured interviews.
- Leaders and team members from across the company were included.
- Interviews were coded in NVivo. The analysis progressed from 335 initial codes to 23 second-level themes and then four higher-level themes.
- The primary qualitative evidence was supplemented with two internal staff surveys about hybrid work.
- Free-text responses were analysed thematically, while a limited count of survey responses added a small quantitative component.
- The secondary data provided triangulation and a limited longitudinal perspective because the surveys occurred two months apart.

The philosophy is interpretivist/social constructionist and the approach inductive. The author was a leader in the organisation.

### Key literature and concepts

The literature review covers:

- **Effective teams:** clear roles and goals, trust, communication, cohesion, accountability, team development, diversity, decision making, and organisational alignment.
- **Team leadership:** the Hill model; Katzenbach and Smith’s practical leadership actions; transformational and transactional leadership; shared leadership.
- **Task and relationship functions:** keeping purpose meaningful, building skills and confidence, removing external obstacles, developing people, and managing team processes.
- **Virtual-team leadership:** technology-mediated communication, shared mental models, role clarity, trust, conflict, meeting practices, coaching, social connection, and well-being.
- **Hybrid leadership:** continually switching modes, matching work location to task, outcome-based goals, face-to-face creativity and coaching, asynchronous work, flexibility, and leader adaptability.
- **Communication norms:** how synchronous and asynchronous channels shape cohesion and effectiveness.

## Main findings

Effective team leadership at Company X was represented by four connected themes:

1. **Communication:** leaders established technology-based communication norms, maintained contact, enabled social interaction, and connected all other leadership activity through clear communication.
2. **Structure and task focus:** leaders clarified roles, maintained goals, assembled the necessary skills, and helped employees establish workable home-and-office routines.
3. **Helping, developing, and connecting with people:** leaders remained approachable, coached and mentored staff, created learning opportunities, showed care, and encouraged social connection.
4. **Supporting team effectiveness:** leaders trusted staff, avoided micromanagement, removed obstacles, encouraged different ideas, and provided help when needed.

Other important findings were:

- Participants reported surprisingly little difference in the fundamentals of leadership across the three work modes.
- Flexibility was the most valued feature of hybrid working; no interviewee wanted an immediate return to five office days.
- Leadership practice varied considerably between teams.
- Some teams were well aligned with the wider organisation while others were not.
- Leaders developed their staff but paid little attention to their own hybrid-leadership development.
- Office-day coordination and matching location to task improved over time but remained inconsistent.
- Team goals were clear, but how team-level success was measured was less clear.
- Internal survey data suggested a perceived decline in productivity, contrary to some external studies.

## Conclusion and recommendations

The dissertation concludes that effective hybrid leadership rests on communication, continuity of structure, task focus, people development and connection, care, trust, and practical support. Hybrid work changes the channels and coordination requirements more than the core purpose of team leadership.

It recommends:

- **Create consistency:** hold regular leadership meetings to share strong practices, improve company-wide communication, and align teams.
- **Use hybrid work deliberately:** coordinate office days, perform tasks requiring rich interaction face-to-face, use remote time for focused work, and reconsider goals for the flexibility offered by hybrid work.
- **Develop leaders:** assess and build the skills leaders themselves need for the hybrid model now that crisis-mode working has ended.

## Limitations and cautions

- The organisation has only 15 employees, all in one UK location and sector.
- Ten interviews provide strong organisational coverage but do not support wider generalisation.
- The author was one of the company’s leaders and supervised some potential participants.
- The study is mainly a snapshot; retrospective comparison across work modes depends on memory.
- The two staff surveys offer only a limited longitudinal component.

## Why read this dissertation

Read this if your topic includes hybrid work, team leadership, employee experience, or thematic analysis. It provides one of the clearest examples in the folder of moving transparently from raw codes to themes and then to an organisation-specific practical model.
