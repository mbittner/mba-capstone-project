# Senior leadership team effectiveness in Nigerian NGOs

**Proposal:** *Senior Leadership Team Effectiveness: A Case Study of NGOs in Nigeria*  
**Author:** Adeboye Oloyede (2020)  
**Best for:** Senior leadership teams, NGO management, team-effectiveness models, and a concurrent mixed-method research design.

## Research problem and purpose

Complex and volatile operating environments require more leadership capacity than a single senior leader can provide. Senior leadership teams collectively shape strategy, decision quality, organisational coordination, and performance.

The proposal argues that these teams frequently fail to realise their potential because of information and interest asymmetries, silo behaviour, resource competition, weak composition, and ineffective group processes. This problem is particularly significant for NGOs, where leadership failure can threaten organisational sustainability and the delivery of essential social interventions.

Research on leadership teams in NGOs—especially in Nigeria—is described as limited compared with research on commercial organisations.

## Research question

> What are the factors that facilitate or inhibit senior leadership team effectiveness in non-governmental organisations in Nigeria?

## Objectives

The proposed study aims to:

1. Critically review literature on leadership-team effectiveness, particularly in NGOs.
2. Profile the characteristics and qualities of the senior leadership teams studied.
3. Draw conclusions and recommend ways to improve senior leadership-team effectiveness.

## How the proposed study is built

### Research design

The proposal adopts pragmatism and a concurrent mixed-method design.

- **Cases:** charitable NGOs at the start-up or expansion stages of their organisational life cycles.
- **Geography:** Lagos State and the Federal Capital Territory, Abuja.
- **Sampling:** purposive selection.
- **Qualitative strand:** semi-structured virtual interviews with CEOs from 15 NGOs, with an expected response rate of 70–80%.
- **Quantitative strand:** an online Google Forms survey of the same organisations’ senior leadership-team members, targeting at least 50% participation from each team.
- **Timing:** interviews and surveys are intended to run concurrently.
- **Analysis:** interviews will be recorded, transcribed, coded, and examined for themes and relationships. Survey findings will be compared with CEO accounts.
- **Triangulation:** the two datasets are intended to deepen understanding and reduce reliance on CEOs’ perspectives alone.
- **Access:** the researcher proposes using an established church network to reach NGO CEOs.

The proposal does not fully specify the survey instrument, variables, statistical analysis, or how qualitative and quantitative results will be integrated.

## Key literature and conceptual framework

### Team effectiveness

The review identifies recurring enablers:

- common purpose and compelling direction;
- appropriate team composition and diversity;
- cohesion and commitment;
- communication and information sharing;
- clear structure, processes, and authority;
- accountability;
- supportive organisational context;
- team coaching;
- constructive leadership style.

Effectiveness includes decision quality, progress toward objectives, team cohesion and commitment, and member development—not performance metrics alone.

### Major team-effectiveness models

The proposal compares:

- **Wageman and colleagues:** a real team, the right people, compelling direction, sound structure, supportive context, and competent coaching.
- **Hackman:** bounded and stable membership, meaningful team tasks, appropriate authority, enabling structure, supportive systems, and coaching.
- **Katzenbach and Smith:** complementary skills, accountability, commitment, performance results, collective work products, and personal growth.
- **Mathieu and colleagues:** team tasks and structure, member characteristics and composition, processes, emergent states, and multiple levels of effectiveness.

The proposal notes that models often emphasise either performance or interaction and may fail to capture effectiveness holistically.

### Team leadership

Leadership is treated as essential to team effectiveness. Leadership style affects decision processes, relationships, coordination, and both direct and indirect team outcomes.

### NGO leadership

The literature highlights:

- personalised or founder-centred leadership;
- skills and leadership-development gaps;
- NGO sustainability;
- cultural influence on participative decision making;
- political, economic, technological, and social pressure;
- the importance of flexibility and distributed attention across senior-team members.

The proposal suggests that NGO leadership deserves context-specific investigation rather than automatic use of commercial-sector findings.

## Expected contribution and outputs

The proposal contains no empirical findings or completed conclusions. It intends to:

- identify factors that strengthen or weaken NGO senior leadership teams;
- profile team composition, qualities, and processes;
- compare CEO perceptions with those of other senior-team members;
- assess how established team-effectiveness models fit Nigerian NGOs;
- produce practical recommendations for team development and NGO sustainability.

## Methodological strengths

- Mixed methods are appropriate for comparing leader and team-member perspectives.
- Concurrent data collection should reduce the risk that one phase unduly shapes the other.
- Studying NGOs at similar life-cycle stages creates some contextual focus.
- Selecting two important Nigerian locations provides access to varied organisations.
- The proposal recognises that team effectiveness includes both performance and group-process outcomes.

## Limitations and points to watch

- The small, purposive sample limits transferability.
- Access through the researcher’s church network may overrepresent connected organisations and create social-desirability pressure.
- CEOs are powerful participants and may present their teams favourably.
- Internet quality may disrupt interviews and survey participation.
- Employees, beneficiaries, donors, volunteers, and board members may assess leadership-team effectiveness differently.
- Focusing on the team’s own perceptions risks common-method and self-assessment bias.
- The proposal does not define how organisational performance will be assessed or linked to team effectiveness.
- Start-up and expansion-stage NGOs may differ considerably despite the shared life-cycle criterion.
- The integration and quantitative-analysis plan needs further detail.

## Why read this proposal

Read this if you want an example of a pragmatic, concurrent mixed-method plan. It offers a useful comparison of major team-effectiveness models and shows how CEO interviews can be triangulated with a broader leadership-team survey. Its main design lesson is the importance of defining integration, outcome measures, and sampling bias before fieldwork begins.
