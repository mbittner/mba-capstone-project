# Dissertation Proposal: Knowledge Transfer in Consulting-Led Transformations

## Title
**How Consulting Firms Can Embed Sustainable Change Through Effective Knowledge Transfer**

---

## 1. Research Background and Context

### 1.1 The Problem in Consulting
A common post-engagement scenario: The consulting project concludes, deliverables are handed over, but the client struggles to maintain momentum or evolve the solution. Technical knowledge sits in documentation nobody reads. The business change the project intended becomes dependent on the consulting team for maintenance or troubleshooting. Within a year, the program is quietly shelved or reduced.

The core issue: Knowledge transfer is treated as a documentation and training task rather than a capability-building process. Consultants train people on the system but don't build deep understanding. They hand off documents but not ownership.

This dissertation examines how consulting firms can embed sustainable change through effective knowledge transfer.

### 1.2 Why This Topic Is Important
1. **Consulting Effectiveness**: Knowledge transfer directly affects whether consulting creates lasting value.
2. **Client Success**: Unsustained change wastes client investment; sustainability is essential.
3. **Business Model Impact**: Improved knowledge transfer reduces post-engagement support costs and improves reputation.
4. **Innovation & Change Theme**: Bridges consulting delivery, capability building, and change sustainability.
5. **Professional Relevance**: As a consulting director, this is directly applicable.

---

## 2. Research Question and Objectives

### 2.1 Primary Research Question
**What knowledge transfer practices most effectively enable insurance clients to sustain and evolve innovation beyond the consulting engagement, and what factors prevent knowledge from sticking?**

### 2.2 Research Objectives

**Objective 1 (Literature):** To critically review literature on knowledge transfer, organizational learning, consulting delivery, capability building, and change sustainability in professional services.

**Objective 2 (Evidence):** To explore how knowledge transfer is actually conducted in consulting engagements, including what works, what fails, and how clients experience the handoff.

**Objective 3 (Analysis):** To identify which knowledge transfer practices correlate with sustained client capability and what barriers prevent effective transfer.

**Objective 4 (Contribution):** To develop a practical knowledge transfer framework for consulting engagements focused on sustainability.

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Research Design
**Longitudinal case study**: Study one or more consulting engagements through knowledge transfer and 6-12 months post-implementation, tracking what was transferred, what stuck, and what fell away.

This approach allows you to:
- Observe knowledge transfer in real time.
- Track sustainability beyond the engagement.
- Understand both consulting and client perspectives on what works.

### 4.2 Data Collection

#### Primary Data: Interviews
- **Sample**: 16-20 interviews including:
  - Consulting engagement leads and delivery team members
  - Client project leads and business owners
  - Client technical or operational staff receiving knowledge
  - Client training or capability leads
  - Follow-up interviews 6-12 months post-engagement

- **Interview Guide Topics**:
  - How knowledge transfer was designed and conducted
  - Training approaches and effectiveness
  - Documentation quality and usability
  - What knowledge stuck vs. was forgotten
  - Barriers to sustained capability
  - Post-engagement support needs
  - What would have improved knowledge transfer

#### Secondary Data: Knowledge Transfer Artifacts
- Training materials and curriculum
- Documentation and handoff materials
- Post-implementation review documents
- Training effectiveness evaluations
- Client capability assessments (if available)

#### Tertiary Data: Post-Implementation Evidence
- System usage patterns
- Help desk tickets or support requests
- Whether recommendations were adopted
- Whether capabilities were maintained without consulting support

### 4.3 Analysis Approach
- **Knowledge Mapping**: What knowledge was intended to transfer vs. what actually transferred.
- **Stickiness Analysis**: Which knowledge stuck vs. what was forgotten, and why.
- **Practice Assessment**: Which transfer practices correlated with sustainable capability.
- **Barrier Analysis**: What prevented effective transfer.
