# Dissertation Proposal: Scaling Innovation from Pilot to Enterprise

## Title
**Leadership Challenges in Scaling Innovation from Pilot Programs to Enterprise-Wide Adoption in Insurance**

---

## 1. Research Background and Context

### 1.1 The Problem in Insurance
Insurance organizations operate in an industry characterized by legacy systems, regulatory constraints, and deeply embedded operating procedures. While many insurers invest in innovation programs, including digital transformation, data analytics, and process automation, these investments often begin as pilot or proof-of-concept projects. However, pilots frequently stall at the transition from experimentation to enterprise-wide adoption.

In a consulting context, this manifests as a common pattern: a pilot shows promise, delivers proof of concept or quick wins, but then fails to gain traction across the organization. The client becomes dependent on the consulting team to maintain it, or the initiative is quietly shelved when resources move elsewhere. The business value imagined at project inception is never realized at scale.

This dissertation examines the leadership, governance, and organizational factors that determine whether innovation scales successfully.

### 1.2 Why This Topic Is Important
1. **High Business Impact**: Scaling is where innovation creates real value. Pilots are experiments; enterprise adoption is where ROI is realized.
2. **Practical Relevance**: Insurance is actively modernizing, so scaling challenges are immediate and widespread.
3. **Consulting Value**: Understanding scaling barriers directly informs consulting delivery and client success.
4. **Personal Professional Context**: As a director at CGI working in insurance, you have access to transformation programs at various stages of scaling.
5. **MBA Alignment**: This topic bridges innovation, change, strategic thinking, culture, and leadership—all core MBA themes.

## 2. Research Question and Objectives

### 2.1 Primary Research Question
**What leadership practices, governance structures, and organizational conditions enable insurance organizations to scale innovation successfully from pilot to enterprise-wide adoption?**

### 2.2 Research Objectives

**Objective 1 (Literature):** To critically review academic literature and practitioner guidance on innovation scaling, pilot-to-scale transitions, change management, governance, organizational capability, and leadership in regulated industries, with particular attention to insurance contexts.

**Objective 2 (Evidence):** To explore the experiences of leaders, sponsors, delivery teams, and operational owners involved in scaling (or attempting to scale) innovation initiatives in insurance organizations, focusing on barriers, enablers, leadership actions, and organizational readiness factors.

**Objective 3 (Analysis):** To analyze the relationship between leadership practices, governance choices, cultural readiness, and the success or failure of innovation scaling.

**Objective 4 (Contribution):** To develop a practical framework or set of recommendations for leaders responsible for scaling innovation from pilot to enterprise in insurance.

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Research Design
**Qualitative Case Study** (primary), potentially supported by **cross-case analysis** if access permits.

This approach is chosen because:
- Scaling is a complex, context-dependent process; qualitative inquiry reveals the "how" and "why."
- Real-world access to insurance transformation programs is limited; a focused case study is more realistic than a large survey.
- The research question emphasizes understanding leadership practices and organizational conditions, not statistical correlation.
- A detailed case study produces rich, actionable insights for practitioners.

### 4.2 Data Collection

#### Primary Data: Interviews
- **Sample**: 12-15 semi-structured interviews across one or two transformation programs, including:
  - Program sponsors or executives
  - Delivery/project leads (internal and consulting)
  - Operational owners (business units affected by scaling)
  - Change managers or adoption leads
  - Cross-functional team members

- **Interview Duration**: 45-60 minutes each
- **Interview Guide**: Organized around themes:
  - The pilot: what was tested, outcomes, initial success factors
  - The decision to scale: who made it, what criteria were used
  - Scaling execution: governance, resource allocation, communication
  - Barriers and enablers: what helped or hindered adoption
  - Leadership practices: what the sponsor/leader did well or could have done differently
  - Organizational readiness: culture, capability, stakeholder alignment

#### Secondary Data: Document Review
- Pilot business case and pilot results documentation
- Scaling/implementation plan and governance artifacts
- Adoption metrics, training materials, communications plans
- Post-implementation reviews or lessons-learned documents
- Organizational charts, decision records

### 4.3 Analysis Approach
- **Coding**: Deductive (using literature-informed codes around governance, leadership, capability, culture) and inductive (remaining open to unexpected themes).
- **Analysis Framework**: Organize codes and themes around the research question: What leadership, governance, and organizational factors enable or constrain scaling?
- **Triangulation**: Compare interview findings with document evidence.
- **Rigor**: Maintain an audit trail; use representative quotations; document interpretive decisions.

### 4.4 Sampling and Access
- **Access Strategy**: Leverage your role at CGI to identify one or two insurance clients with a scaling initiative at a suitable stage (either in progress or completed within the past 12-18 months).
- **Feasibility Test**: Before formal ethics submission, confirm:
  - Permission from client leadership to discuss the program
  - Willingness of 12+ participants to be interviewed
  - Access to key documents (non-confidential elements)
  - Realistic timeline for interviews and analysis

### 4.5 Limitations and Boundaries
- **Single-case or two-case design** limits generalizability but increases depth and practitioner relevance.
- **Retrospective reporting** (if the scaling occurred in the past) may introduce recall bias; this is mitigated by document review.
- **Ethics boundary**: Only non-confidential aspects of the program will be discussed; participant anonymity and client confidentiality will be protected.

## 5. Ethical Considerations

### 5.1 Research Ethics Route
This study qualifies for the **block/low-risk ethics approval route** under York St John University guidelines:
- Participants are adults (insurance professionals) who can freely give informed consent.
- No sensitive personal data (health, criminal, political beliefs) are collected.
- The study uses standard semi-structured interviews and document review.
- Participants face minimal risk; interviews are work-related and confidential.

### 5.2 Key Safeguards
- Participant information sheets will explain the study purpose, confidentiality protections, and withdrawal rights.
- Signed consent forms will be collected before each interview.
- Data will be anonymized: participant names and identifiable organizational details will be removed from transcripts and analysis.
- Audio recordings will be encrypted and destroyed after transcription.
- Client organization identity may be disclosed only with explicit written permission.
- All data will be stored securely and access restricted to the researcher and supervisor.
- GDPR and York St John University data protection policies will be followed.

### 5.3 Gatekeeping and Approval
- Written permission from the client organization (or a designated gatekeeper) will be obtained before recruitment.
- The supervisor will review all participant-facing documents (information sheet, consent form) before use.
