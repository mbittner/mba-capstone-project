# HRD strategies for managerial adaptability

**Proposal:** *HRD Strategies that foster and enable the adaptability of managers within rapidly changing for-profit organisations in the Netherlands*  
**Author:** Sandra Ahlers (2018)  
**Best for:** Research on adaptability, management development, organisational change, and qualitative interviews with HRD professionals.

## Research problem and purpose

Rapid organisational change increases the need for managers who can notice, interpret, and respond to changing conditions. Managers matter not only because they must adapt personally, but because their behaviour can encourage or inhibit adaptability among employees.

The proposal argues that human resource development (HRD)—including training, assessment, learning, and development programmes—can build adaptive capability. However, the relationship is not simple. Adaptability also depends on personal resources, leadership, organisational systems, culture, structure, and the change environment.

The study therefore seeks to identify which HRD strategies are perceived to develop managerial adaptability effectively in rapidly changing, for-profit organisations in the Netherlands.

## Research question

> What HRD strategies enable and foster the adaptability of managers within fast-changing for-profit organisations in the Netherlands?

## Objectives

The proposed research aims to:

1. Analyse literature on individual adaptability, its relevance in rapidly changing organisations, and the factors that affect a manager’s capacity to adapt.
2. Evaluate HRD strategies and the extent to which they can develop managerial adaptability in Dutch for-profit organisations.
3. Recommend HRD strategies that can enable and foster managers’ adaptability.

## How the proposed study is built

### Research design

The proposal sets out an exploratory, qualitative, cross-organisational study.

- **Philosophy:** interpretivism, because the research seeks to understand how HRD professionals interpret adaptability and development in their organisational settings.
- **Approach:** inductive. Findings from literature and interviews are intended to support broader conclusions and possibly new HRD propositions.
- **Sample:** 12 HRD professionals working in different fast-changing, for-profit organisations in the Netherlands.
- **Sampling logic:** participants are selected for their knowledge of HRD practices and managerial development across multiple organisational contexts.
- **Data collection:** semi-structured interviews about strategies used, their intended effects, and how effective participants perceive them to be.
- **Analysis:** coding and thematic analysis based on Braun and Clarke.
- **Reflexivity:** the author acknowledges that over 20 years of HRD experience will shape interpretation and proposes continual reflection and discussion with other professionals.

The planned design gathers expert practitioner perceptions. It does not directly measure managers’ adaptability before and after an intervention.

## Key literature and conceptual framework

The proposal combines four main bodies of literature:

### Individual adaptability

Van Dam’s multidimensional definition is central. Adaptability is treated as potential derived from:

- **cognitive resources:** noticing changes and revising thoughts or assumptions;
- **affective resources:** emotional recovery, resilience, and the ability to cope;
- **behavioural resources:** adjusting actions to meet new demands.

This is broader than definitions that focus only on observable adaptive behaviour.

### Adaptive leadership and managerial influence

Adaptive leadership involves changing behaviour to suit a changing situation. Managers also model responses to change and influence employee readiness, confidence, and engagement.

### Personal resources and related concepts

The literature links adaptability to:

- self-efficacy;
- resilience and coping;
- emotional intelligence;
- openness and willingness to change;
- readiness for change;
- flexibility;
- locus of control, while distinguishing it from self-efficacy;
- meaning-making and access to change-related information.

### Human resource development

HRD is defined as intentional activity that increases knowledge, skill, and capability in support of organisational strategy. The proposal stresses that HRD should:

- align with business strategy;
- start from evidence about what enables or blocks adaptability;
- avoid assuming employees are inherently resistant;
- incorporate the beliefs and experiences of change recipients;
- operate alongside supportive leadership, culture, structures, and systems.

## Expected contribution and outputs

As a proposal, this document contains no empirical findings or final conclusions. The planned research is expected to:

- clarify how HRD practitioners define managerial adaptability;
- identify development strategies used across Dutch organisations;
- compare practitioner perceptions of their effectiveness;
- connect HRD interventions to cognitive, affective, and behavioural adaptability;
- produce recommendations for HRD professionals;
- generate propositions that could later be tested deductively.

## Methodological strengths

- The research question, sample, and qualitative method are well aligned.
- Comparing several organisations should reveal a broader range of HRD practices than a single case.
- Semi-structured interviews allow practitioners to explain context and unintended effects.
- The proposal acknowledges researcher subjectivity rather than presenting interpretation as neutral.
- The multidimensional adaptability framework provides a useful organising concept.

## Limitations and points to watch

- Interviewing only HRD professionals provides one organisational perspective. Managers receiving the interventions may evaluate them differently.
- Perceived effectiveness is not the same as demonstrated improvement in adaptability.
- The proposal does not specify how “fast-changing” organisations will be defined or selected.
- Twelve interviews can produce depth but will not support statistical generalisation.
- Organisational culture, structure, systems, and leadership are acknowledged but mostly placed outside the study’s scope.
- The literature review does not aim to catalogue every HRD strategy.
- The researcher’s professional experience is valuable but may favour familiar HRD explanations.

## Why read this proposal

Read this if your topic involves management development, adaptability, or HR’s role in organisational change. It is a concise example of narrowing a broad change topic to one population, one organisational mechanism, and one national context. Its main value is the connection between a multidimensional definition of adaptability and a practical interview study of HRD strategies.
