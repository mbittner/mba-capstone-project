# Dissertation Proposal: Innovation Portfolio Management

## Title
**Strategic Governance of Innovation Portfolios in Insurance Firms**

---

## 1. Research Background and Context

### 1.1 The Problem in Insurance
Insurance organizations typically run multiple innovation initiatives simultaneously without a clear portfolio logic. Projects compete for resources, stakeholders don't know which initiatives matter most, and priorities shift with each leadership change. The result: resources are spread thinly, some initiatives languish, and others consume resources without clear value.

The challenge: How do leaders prioritize competing innovation initiatives to maximize business value while managing risk and maintaining organizational focus?

This dissertation examines how insurance firms govern innovation portfolios and what governance approaches most effectively balance incremental and transformational innovation.

### 1.2 Why This Topic Is Important
1. **Strategic Thinking Theme**: Core MBA topic; resource allocation is a fundamental leadership challenge.
2. **Business Value**: Portfolio prioritization directly affects return on innovation investment.
3. **Leadership & Governance**: Requires leaders to make tough tradeoff decisions with stakeholder alignment.
4. **Consulting Application**: Portfolio governance is a common consulting engagement.
5. **Complex but Solvable**: The problem is real but addressable through governance discipline.

## 2. Research Question and Objectives

### 2.1 Primary Research Question
**What governance approaches and decision criteria do insurance leaders use to prioritize innovation initiatives, and which frameworks most effectively balance incremental and transformational innovation while ensuring resource optimization?**

### 2.2 Research Objectives

**Objective 1 (Literature):** To critically review literature on portfolio management, innovation governance, resource allocation, and decision-making frameworks in strategy and innovation contexts.

**Objective 2 (Evidence):** To explore how innovation portfolios are actually governed in insurance, including governance structures, prioritization criteria, and how decisions are made and enforced.

**Objective 3 (Analysis):** To assess which governance approaches and decision criteria most effectively balance portfolio objectives and improve business outcomes.

**Objective 4 (Contribution):** To develop a practical governance framework for innovation portfolio management in insurance.

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Research Design
**Case study of portfolio governance processes**: Study how one or two insurance organizations govern their innovation portfolios, from prioritization through decision-making and resource allocation.

### 4.2 Data Collection

#### Primary Data: Interviews
- **Sample**: 14-18 interviews including:
  - Chief innovation officer or equivalent
  - Portfolio or strategy leaders
  - Funding or capital allocation leaders
  - Business unit or division heads
  - Program sponsors
  - Risk or compliance leads influencing decisions

- **Interview Guide Topics**:
  - How portfolio governance is structured
  - What criteria prioritization decisions use
  - How incremental vs. transformational investments are balanced
  - How stakeholders are engaged
  - How decisions are made and enforced
  - Satisfaction with current governance
  - Post-decision outcomes

#### Secondary Data: Governance Documents
- Portfolio governance policies and frameworks
- Prioritization criteria or stage-gate processes
- Portfolio review materials (if not confidential)
- Investment decisions and rationale
- Resource allocation decisions

#### Tertiary Data: Process Observation
- Observe portfolio review meetings if access permits.

### 4.3 Analysis Approach
- **Governance Mapping**: Document decision structure, criteria, stakeholder roles.
- **Prioritization Analysis**: Extract what criteria actually drive decisions vs. stated criteria.
- **Outcome Tracking**: Link decisions to business results.
- **Comparative Analysis**: If two organizations, compare governance approaches.
