# Leading an organisation during the COVID-19 crisis

**Dissertation:** *Leading an Organisation During the COVID-19 Crisis*  
**Author:** Volha Mitra (2021)  
**Best for:** Crisis leadership, communication, employee security, collaboration, innovation, and remote working in a medium-sized business.

## Research question

> What leadership processes and behaviours can sustain performance during the current economic crisis within the case study company?

The study focuses on human relationships rather than the technical or operational mechanics of crisis management.

## Objectives

The dissertation aims to:

1. Critically review crisis-leadership literature.
2. Gather and analyse information about leadership at “Company A” during COVID-19.
3. Identify processes and behaviours perceived to sustain performance and compare them with relevant theories.

## How the dissertation was built

### Research design

This is an exploratory, qualitative, cross-sectional case study of Company A, a 42-person distributor of premium personal-care and fragrance brands across the Gulf Cooperation Council, headquartered in the UAE.

- Twelve employees formed a purposive, maximum-variation sample—29% of the workforce.
- Participants varied by department, nationality, gender, tenure, and experience of the crisis.
- Two participants had worked elsewhere during the first crisis stage and provided limited comparative evidence.
- One-to-one Microsoft Teams interviews lasted 30–60 minutes, averaging 47 minutes.
- Questions examined employee challenges, organisational response, and perceptions of leadership. Appreciative-inquiry and behavioural-interview techniques were used for sensitive topics.
- Interviews were recorded; data was analysed through Braun and Clarke’s reflexive thematic analysis.
- The analysis was abductive, moving repeatedly between emerging themes and crisis-leadership theory.

The researcher was an employee of Company A, improving access but introducing possible political, response, and interpretation bias.

### Key literature and concepts

The conceptual base includes:

- **Crisis definitions and stages:** low-probability, high-impact, ambiguous threats; pre-crisis, response, and post-crisis phases; COVID-19 as extreme and sustained crisis.
- **Crisis as threat and opportunity:** retrenchment, perseverance, innovation, or exit.
- **Directive versus participative leadership:** threat-rigidity, centralised command, fast decisions, collaboration, shared leadership, and the crisis-leadership dilemma.
- **Transformational leadership:** idealised influence, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, and individualised consideration.
- **Urgency and adaptability:** anticipate, decide, reassess, learn, and adjust continuously.
- **Transparent communication:** bounded optimism, credibility, stability, trust, and honest explanation of difficult decisions.
- **Motivating Language Theory:** direction-giving, meaning-making, and empathy.
- **Employee psychological needs:** job security, compensation, support, compassion, and fairness.
- **Innovation and collaboration:** information sharing, expertise, intrinsic motivation, creativity, and employee voice.
- **Remote and virtual-team leadership:** trust, culture, communication, shared leadership, work-life boundaries, and alignment.

## Main findings

Twelve themes were grouped into three central areas.

### Organisational preparedness and survival

- Company A was unprepared and did not recognise or act during the pre-crisis stage.
- When revenue collapsed, the principal leader reacted urgently and autocratically, closing most operations and imposing unpaid leave or half pay.
- The decision may have supported survival, but the lack of consultation prevented employees from contributing expertise and possible alternatives.
- Remote work was not supported with dedicated policies, routines, alignment, or leadership practices.

### Crisis communication and employee security

- Lack of clear, transparent, empathetic communication was the most important weakness.
- Employees placed job security above other concerns.
- Lower-paid employees who received clear information about half pay and job continuity felt more stable than employees left on unpaid leave without updates.
- Employees without communication and support experienced panic, resentment, abandonment, and reduced trust.
- Meaning-making, direction-giving, and empathy were used inconsistently.

### Collaboration and innovation

- After approximately two months, the leader began listening to the team and the organisation shifted toward more collaborative behaviour.
- Employees shared knowledge, developed digital capabilities, opened new revenue opportunities, and drove new ideas.
- Innovation helped the company recover, expand its brand portfolio, and increase its client base.
- Creativity was motivated partly by employees’ urgent need to return to paid work rather than purely by intrinsic engagement.

## Conclusion and recommendations

Company A recovered quickly and avoided layoffs or post-crisis resignations, but commercial survival did not mean the leadership response was good practice. Trust and loyalty may have suffered lasting damage.

The dissertation recommends:

1. **Prepare and act with urgency:** develop crisis strategies and systems, then reassess them annually.
2. **Promote collaboration:** reduce unnecessary autocratic behaviour, draw on expertise throughout the organisation, and encourage shared leadership and innovation.
3. **Communicate transparently:** use direction-giving, meaning-making, and empathy, especially when stability is threatened.
4. **Formalise remote work:** establish practices, procedures, virtual-team alignment, and leadership routines for future disruption.

## Limitations and cautions

- The study concerns one organisation and 12 participants.
- COVID-19 research was still developing when the dissertation was written.
- Insider status may have influenced what participants disclosed and how themes were interpreted.
- Findings are perception-based and cannot be claimed statistically.
- The study cannot establish whether a more participative initial response would have produced better financial results.

## Why read this dissertation

Read this if you are interested in the tension between decisive action and participation during crisis. Its strongest contribution is showing that business recovery, employee experience, trust, and leadership quality can point in different directions.
