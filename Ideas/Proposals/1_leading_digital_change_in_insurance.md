# Dissertation Proposal: Leading Digital Change in Insurance

## Title
**How Leadership Influences Successful Digital Transformation in Insurance Consulting**

---

## 1. Research Background and Context

### 1.1 The Problem in Insurance
Insurance organizations are under sustained pressure to modernize legacy systems, adopt cloud technology, implement data analytics, and automate workflows. These digital transformation initiatives are essential for competitiveness but carry significant risk: weak leadership, poor change management, and unclear priorities often result in adoption failure, cost overruns, and abandoned projects.

In a consulting context, this challenge manifests as a leadership problem. Technology vendors can provide solutions, but the insurance organization must adopt and embed them. Success depends heavily on how leaders communicate the transformation, manage resistance, engage stakeholders, and maintain momentum through long implementation cycles.

This dissertation examines which leadership behaviors and practices actually enable successful digital transformation in insurance.

### 1.2 Why This Topic Is Important
1. **Urgent Industry Problem**: Digital transformation is a top priority for insurance but has a mixed success rate.
2. **Leadership-Centric Lens**: Most digital projects fail due to leadership, change, and adoption issues—not technology.
3. **Consulting Relevance**: Understanding leadership effectiveness directly improves consulting delivery and client outcomes.
4. **Career Alignment**: As a consulting director working with insurance, this is directly applicable to your work.
5. **MBA Connections**: Bridges innovation, change, leadership, and strategic thinking.

## 2. Research Question and Objectives

### 2.1 Primary Research Question
**What leadership behaviors, communication practices, and decision-making approaches most effectively drive technology adoption and business value realization in insurance digital transformation programs?**

### 2.2 Research Objectives

**Objective 1 (Literature):** To critically review academic literature on digital transformation, technology adoption, change leadership, stakeholder engagement, and implementation effectiveness, with particular focus on regulated industries and insurance contexts.

**Objective 2 (Evidence):** To explore leaders' and employees' experiences during digital transformation initiatives, focusing on leadership practices, communication effectiveness, resistance patterns, and adoption outcomes.

**Objective 3 (Analysis):** To analyze the relationship between specific leadership behaviors and digital adoption success, identifying which practices correlate with sustained business value realization.

**Objective 4 (Contribution):** To develop a practical framework of leadership practices for digital transformation success in insurance.

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Research Design
**Mixed-method case study**: Qualitative interviews (primary) plus optional quantitative survey (secondary) of adoption metrics.

This approach allows you to:
- Understand the "why" behind adoption success/failure through interviews.
- Optionally triangulate with adoption metrics (e.g., system usage, business value realization).
- Maintain depth and richness while adding some measurable dimension.

### 4.2 Data Collection

#### Primary Data: Interviews
- **Sample**: 15-20 semi-structured interviews across one or two digital programs, including:
  - Senior sponsor or executive
  - Program director/delivery lead
  - Business/operational leaders
  - Change managers
  - End users or adoption leads
  - Consulting partners (if external delivery)

- **Interview Guide Topics**:
  - Leadership vision and communication
  - Change strategy and resistance management
  - Stakeholder engagement and decision-making
  - Barriers and enablers
  - Adoption outcomes and perceived business value

#### Secondary Data: Adoption Metrics (Optional)
- System usage rates or log data
- Training completion rates
- Post-implementation review documents
- Business case and realized benefits tracking

#### Tertiary Data: Document Review
- Transformation communications (emails, announcements, training materials)
- Governance meeting minutes
- Risk and issue logs
- Program closure reports

### 4.3 Analysis Approach
- **Coding**: Deductive (leadership literature codes) and inductive (emergent themes).
- **Framework**: Link identified leadership behaviors to adoption and value outcomes.
- **Triangulation**: Compare interview accounts with adoption data and documents.

## 5. Ethical Considerations

This study qualifies for the **block/low-risk ethics route**:
- Participants are insurance professionals with full capacity to consent.
- Standard interviews on work topics (no sensitive personal data).
- Minimal risk; interviews are confidential.

**Key safeguards**:
- Participant information sheet and signed consent forms.
- Data anonymization (remove names, identifiable program details).
- Secure storage, GDPR compliance.
- Gatekeeper approval from client organization.
