# Dissertation Proposal: Resistance to Change in Regulated Industries

## Title
**Understanding Resistance to Innovation in Insurance and How Leaders Can Reduce It**

---

## 1. Research Background and Context

### 1.1 The Problem in Insurance
Resistance to change is pervasive in insurance, yet it is often treated as employee obstruction rather than legitimate feedback. The reality: resistance in insurance typically has deep roots—compliance concerns, risk aversion, job security worries, or lack of clarity about why the change is necessary.

Insurance culture amplifies resistance: employees are trained to be cautious, to follow process, to escalate uncertainties. That same carefulness, when applied to change, becomes "resistance." Leaders often view resistance as something to overcome rather than understand.

This dissertation examines what causes resistance in insurance, what legitimate concerns underlie it, and what leadership approaches actually reduce it.

### 1.2 Why This Topic Is Important
1. **Ubiquitous Problem**: Every transformation in insurance faces resistance; understanding it creates value immediately.
2. **Leadership & Change Theme**: Central to both leadership and innovation/change modules.
3. **Practical Impact**: Reducing resistance improves adoption and transformation success.
4. **Consulting Application**: Understanding resistance helps consultants advise clients and manage engagement dynamics.
5. **Psychological Depth**: Moves beyond surface-level "managing resistance" to understanding human response to change.

---

## 2. Research Question and Objectives

### 2.1 Primary Research Question
**What are the primary sources and underlying causes of resistance to innovation in insurance organizations, and what leadership and communication approaches most effectively address legitimate concerns and improve adoption?**

### 2.2 Research Objectives

**Objective 1 (Literature):** To critically review literature on resistance to change, its psychological and organizational roots, and effective approaches to engagement and communication, with attention to regulated and risk-averse organizational contexts.

**Objective 2 (Evidence):** To explore resistance from multiple perspectives—resisters, leaders, and change managers—to understand both surface objections and underlying concerns.

**Objective 3 (Analysis):** To categorize types of resistance, identify which represent legitimate concerns versus ungrounded fear, and assess which leadership responses are most effective.

**Objective 4 (Contribution):** To develop a framework for leaders on understanding and addressing resistance in insurance.

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Research Design
**Qualitative case study with multi-stakeholder perspective**: Study a specific change initiative, interviewing both those who resisted and those leading the change to understand roots of resistance and effectiveness of responses.

This approach allows you to:
- Understand resistance from the perspective of those experiencing it.
- Assess which leadership responses worked and which didn't.
- Identify patterns in what triggers resistance in insurance.

### 4.2 Data Collection

#### Primary Data: Interviews
- **Sample**: 18-24 interviews including:
  - Employees who resisted (and their reasons)
  - Leaders driving the change
  - Change managers or adoption leads
  - Middle managers bridging resistance and leadership
  - HR or organizational development leads

- **Interview Guide Topics**:
  - What specifically caused resistance
  - Legitimate vs. unfounded concerns
  - How resistance was expressed
  - What leadership/communication helped address it
  - What made it worse
  - How concerns were (or weren't) resolved

#### Secondary Data: Communications and Documentation
- Change announcements and communications
- Town halls, Q&A sessions, training materials
- Resistance escalations or feedback
- Post-implementation reviews
- Adoption metrics by department (to see where resistance was strongest)

#### Tertiary Data: Observation
- Observe change events or team meetings where resistance surfaces, if access permits.

### 4.3 Analysis Approach
- **Resistance Categorization**: Classify types of resistance (lack of clarity, job security concern, workload concern, misalignment with values, etc.).
- **Thematic Analysis**: Identify patterns in what caused resistance and what responses worked.
- **Comparative Analysis**: Analyze differences between groups that adopted vs. resisted.

## 5. Ethical Considerations

**Low-risk route**:
- Standard interviews about workplace experience during change.
- No sensitive personal data.
- Participants are adults who can consent.

**Key safeguards**:
- Protect confidentiality of those who resisted; assure no retaliation.
- Anonymize resisters and leaders.
- Secure data storage; GDPR compliance.
- Frame the study as learning, not evaluation.
