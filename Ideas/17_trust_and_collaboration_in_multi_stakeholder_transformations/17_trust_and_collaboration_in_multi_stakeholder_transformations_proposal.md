# Dissertation Proposal: Trust and Collaboration in Multi-Stakeholder Transformations

## Title
**Building Trust and Collaboration Among Multiple Stakeholders in Insurance Transformation Programs**

---

## 1. Research Background and Context

### 1.1 The Problem in Insurance
Insurance transformations typically involve multiple stakeholders: internal teams (business, technology, operations), external partners (consulting firms, vendors, InsurTech startups), regulators, customers, and sometimes shareholders. Each stakeholder has different agendas, risk tolerance, and success criteria. Building trust and collaboration across these groups is difficult but critical to transformation success.

When trust breaks down, transformations stall: projects fall behind, quality suffers, people disengage, and costs spiral. Yet transformations are often structured in ways that undermine trust (competitive vendor selection, separate business-technology teams, limited transparency).

This dissertation examines what leaders do to build and sustain trust and collaboration across diverse stakeholder groups during complex transformations.

### 1.2 Why This Topic Is Important
1. **Critical Success Factor**: Stakeholder collaboration directly affects transformation outcomes.
2. **Leadership Practice**: Building trust requires specific, observable leadership behaviors and organizational practices.
3. **Complex Context**: Insurance transformations involve diverse, sometimes conflicting stakeholders; makes the challenge interesting.
4. **Measurable**: Can assess collaboration effectiveness, trust levels, and business outcomes.
5. **Actionable**: Specific practices can improve stakeholder collaboration and trust.

---

## 2. Research Question and Objectives

### 2.1 Primary Research Question
**What leadership practices and organizational structures most effectively build and maintain trust and collaboration among diverse stakeholders during insurance transformation programs, and how do leaders navigate and reconcile conflicting stakeholder interests?**

### 2.2 Research Objectives

**Objective 1 (Literature):** To critically review literature on trust, collaboration, stakeholder management, organizational partnerships, and program governance in complex multi-party initiatives.

**Objective 2 (Evidence):** To explore how insurance transformation leaders build and sustain trust and collaboration across diverse stakeholders, and what practices work (or fail).

**Objective 3 (Analysis):** To identify which leadership and governance approaches most effectively enable stakeholder collaboration and reconcile conflicting interests.

**Objective 4 (Contribution):** To develop practical guidance for building and sustaining multi-stakeholder collaboration in insurance transformations.

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Research Design
**Case study of multi-stakeholder collaboration in transformation**: Study one or two insurance transformation programs involving multiple stakeholders to understand how leaders build and sustain trust and collaboration.

### 4.2 Data Collection

#### Primary Data: Interviews
- **Sample**: 18-20 interviews across multiple stakeholder groups including:
  - Transformation sponsor/executive
  - Transformation program manager/director
  - Internal team leaders (business, technology, operations)
  - Consulting partner leads
  - Vendor/partner leaders
  - Individual contributors from different stakeholder groups
  - People experiencing trust breakdowns and collaboration challenges

- **Interview Guide Topics**:
  - Stakeholder groups involved and their agendas
  - How stakeholder interests align or conflict
  - Examples of trust and collaboration (and what enabled them)
  - Examples of trust breakdown or poor collaboration (and why)
  - Leadership practices that build trust across groups
  - Governance structures that enable or hinder collaboration
  - How conflicts between stakeholders are resolved
  - Transparency and communication practices
  - Lessons learned about managing multiple stakeholders

#### Secondary Data: Program Governance and Communication Documents
- Program governance structure and decision-making process
- Stakeholder communication and engagement plans
- Program milestone achievements and delays
- Documentation of conflicts and resolutions
- Meeting minutes that reveal collaboration quality

#### Tertiary Data: Observation and Collaboration Artifacts
- Observe key program meetings to assess collaboration quality.
- Review communication artifacts (emails, status reports, newsletters) for tone and transparency.

### 4.3 Analysis Approach
- **Stakeholder Mapping**: Document stakeholder groups, agendas, and relationships.
- **Trust and Collaboration Patterns**: Extract examples and mechanisms of trust and breakdown.
- **Leadership Practice Analysis**: Identify practices leaders use to build trust and collaboration.
- **Governance Assessment**: Evaluate how governance structure supports or undermines collaboration.

## 5. Ethical Considerations

**Low-risk route**:
- Interviews about program governance, stakeholder management, and collaboration.
- Standard consent and confidentiality.

**Key safeguards**:
- Anonymize organization, people, and conflicts.
- Avoid proprietary program details or competitive vendor information.
- Secure data storage; GDPR compliance.
- Be careful with sensitive topics (conflicts, trust breakdown); ensure participants feel safe.
