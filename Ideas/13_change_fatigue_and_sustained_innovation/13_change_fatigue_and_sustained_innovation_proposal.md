# Dissertation Proposal: Change Fatigue and Sustained Innovation

## Title
**Managing Change Fatigue While Sustaining Innovation Momentum in Insurance Organizations**

---

## 1. Research Background and Context

### 1.1 The Problem in Insurance
Insurance organizations typically run too many change initiatives simultaneously. Employees face: a digital transformation program, a regulatory compliance overhaul, an organizational restructure, and ongoing innovation projects. Change fatigue sets in: people become cynical, stop engaging, or leave. This makes it harder to sustain the pace of innovation that competitive pressure demands.

The tension: Leaders need continuous innovation to stay competitive, but change fatigue erodes the organizational capacity to change well. How do leaders pace change and sustain innovation momentum while protecting people from burnout?

This dissertation examines how insurance leaders manage this tension and what approaches successfully sustain innovation momentum despite change saturation.

### 1.2 Why This Topic Is Important
1. **Real Business Problem**: Change fatigue is recognized but rarely well-managed; affects innovation success directly.
2. **Leadership Practice**: Requires leaders to make difficult tradeoff decisions about pacing and prioritization.
3. **Innovation & Change Theme**: Core MBA topics applied to a complex, modern challenge.
4. **Measurable**: Can assess fatigue (engagement surveys, retention) and innovation momentum (adoption rates, idea flow).
5. **Actionable**: Specific practices can improve pacing and sustain momentum.

---

## 2. Research Question and Objectives

### 2.1 Primary Research Question
**How do insurance leaders manage change fatigue while maintaining innovation momentum, and what organizational practices most effectively sustain innovation participation despite high rates of organizational change?**

### 2.2 Research Objectives

**Objective 1 (Literature):** To critically review literature on change management, change fatigue, organizational capacity for change, innovation diffusion, and managing competing initiatives.

**Objective 2 (Evidence):** To explore how insurance leaders experience and respond to change fatigue and what strategies they use to maintain innovation engagement during periods of high change.

**Objective 3 (Analysis):** To identify which practices most effectively sustain innovation momentum despite change saturation and how organizations balance multiple initiatives.

**Objective 4 (Contribution):** To develop practical guidance for managing change pacing and sustaining innovation in high-change environments.

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Research Design
**Exploratory case study of change management and innovation sustainability**: Study one or two insurance organizations managing multiple concurrent change initiatives to understand how they pace change and sustain innovation participation.

### 4.2 Data Collection

#### Primary Data: Interviews
- **Sample**: 16-18 interviews including:
  - Executives setting change strategy and pace
  - Change and innovation leaders
  - Team leaders and managers
  - Individual contributors experiencing change fatigue
  - People actively participating in innovation (and why)
  - People who have disengaged (and why)

- **Interview Guide Topics**:
  - How many concurrent change initiatives are happening
  - Symptoms of change fatigue observed
  - How change is paced and prioritized
  - How innovation is encouraged despite change
  - Strategies to maintain engagement
  - Success stories of sustained innovation during high change
  - Failures or missteps

#### Secondary Data: Engagement and Innovation Metrics
- Employee engagement survey results (trend over change periods)
- Innovation participation rates (ideas submitted, pilots run)
- Retention rates and turnover trends
- Change program timelines and overlap
- Documentation of change pacing decisions

#### Tertiary Data: Observation and Change Artifacts
- Observe team meetings to assess engagement levels.
- Review change communications for messaging about pacing and support.

### 4.3 Analysis Approach
- **Timeline Mapping**: Map concurrent change initiatives to understand overlap and pacing.
- **Fatigue Indicators**: Identify indicators of change fatigue in interviews and metrics.
- **Engagement Strategies**: Extract strategies used to sustain innovation participation.
- **Correlation Analysis**: Link pacing practices to engagement and innovation outcomes.

## 5. Ethical Considerations

**Low-risk route**:
- Interviews about change management, innovation participation, and employee experience.
- Standard consent and confidentiality.

**Key safeguards**:
- Anonymize organization and participants.
- Avoid identifying individuals who have left or disengaged.
- Secure data storage; GDPR compliance.
- Sensitive topic: ensure participants feel safe speaking candidly about fatigue.
