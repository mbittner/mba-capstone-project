# Value creation in non-profit member organisations

**Dissertation:** *Value creation in non-profit member organisations: Building the membership base and improving the brand through social media*  
**Author:** Sue Arundale (2012)  
**Best for:** Understanding how a membership organisation can investigate its value proposition, member loyalty, brand, and the effect of social media through a mixed-method study.

## Research question

The study asks whether a pseudonymous non-profit membership organisation, “The Foundation,” creates enough value to sustain membership as its principal source of revenue. It also asks whether social media is replacing the organisation’s traditional role in knowledge sharing, benchmarking, and relationship building.

## Objectives

The dissertation aims to:

1. Review strategy, organisational capability, innovation, and non-profit membership literature.
2. Assess whether social media has substituted for the Foundation’s core competencies, particularly knowledge sharing, benchmarking, and member relationships.
3. Determine whether supporting the Foundation’s cause remains a reason for member loyalty.
4. Identify changes that could strengthen member value, membership levels, and the brand.

## How the dissertation was built

### Research design

This is a cross-sectional, mixed-method case study conducted in late 2011.

- The population comprised 465 current members and 83 organisations that had recently ended their membership.
- Invitations were sent to 428 current and 39 former members.
- An online questionnaire was piloted twice and produced 126 complete responses: 118 current members, six former members, and two respondents whose status was unclear.
- Eight current or former member representatives took part in semi-structured telephone interviews. Interviews were recorded and transcribed.
- Survey results were analysed descriptively, while interview material was grouped into themes. The two sources were compared for triangulation.

The researcher was an organisational insider. This improved access and contextual understanding but introduced a risk of interpretation bias.

### Key literature and concepts

The literature review combines several strands:

- **Customer value and the value proposition:** how organisations identify, deliver, and communicate benefits that customers or members value.
- **Strategy and strategic position:** environmental analysis, competitive advantage, organisational resources, capabilities, and core competencies.
- **Non-profit performance:** the difficulty of measuring success when the mission, rather than profit, is central; Collins’ social-sector adaptation of *Good to Great* is important here.
- **Brand and loyalty:** emotional commitment, identification with a cause, recognition, and the relationship between perceived value and continued membership.
- **Innovation and business models:** redesigning how value is created rather than merely adding services.
- **Member engagement and communities:** knowledge exchange, benchmarking, relationships, and participation.
- **Social media and social networking:** whether open online networks disintermediate membership organisations or provide an additional engagement channel.

The literature gap is the limited research on business-to-business-style, non-profit membership organisations operating in a social-media environment.

## Main findings

- Membership organisations still have a meaningful role in knowledge sharing and relationship brokering.
- Social media makes direct connection and information sharing easier, but respondents did not generally see it as a replacement for the Foundation’s specialist knowledge, personal relationships, and curated network. It is better understood as a complement and amplifier.
- Supporting the cause—business excellence—remained a meaningful reason for loyalty and affiliation.
- The Foundation created value for many members, but the value proposition was not sufficiently clear, consistently delivered, or well communicated.
- Personal contacts, visibility, benchmarking, research, case studies, recognition, and access to other members were among the most valued benefits.
- Former members were less positive than current members, but only six former members completed the questionnaire, so that comparison is weak.
- The decline in membership suggested a genuine value-delivery problem even though respondents still believed the organisation could be valuable.

## Conclusion and recommendations

The Foundation could sustain a membership-based model, but it needed to make the relationship between its mission, services, and member benefits much more explicit.

The dissertation recommends:

- defining and communicating a sharper member value proposition;
- strengthening personal, member-focused support;
- increasing member visibility and recognition;
- expanding useful benchmarking, research, and case-study material;
- monitoring social media and participating intelligently in relevant conversations;
- using social platforms to extend, rather than abandon, the Foundation’s relationship-based strengths;
- considering collaboration and strategic repositioning where these strengthen the brand and member offer.

The author does not conclude that radical reinvention is required. Better execution, clearer positioning, and stronger engagement may deliver more value than wholesale organisational change.

## Limitations and cautions

- The former-member response was extremely small.
- Current respondents may be disproportionately loyal or engaged.
- Findings reflect perceptions at one point in time rather than observed renewal behaviour over time.
- The insider researcher may have influenced interpretation.
- International membership and language differences may have affected responses.

The findings are therefore useful for organisational diagnosis but should not be treated as proof that specific changes would cause membership growth.

## Why read this dissertation

Read this if your research involves customer or stakeholder value, professional associations, non-profits, brand loyalty, or social media. Its strongest feature is the clear connection between a strategic problem, a member survey, follow-up interviews, and practical recommendations.
