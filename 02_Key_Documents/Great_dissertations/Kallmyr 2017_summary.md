# Transactional versus transformational leadership

**Dissertation:** *Transactional vs Transformational Leadership: How Do Leaders Support Innovation and Change*  
**Author:** Mats Kallmyr (2017)  
**Best for:** Comparing leadership styles in organisational innovation and change through interviews with middle managers.

## Research question

> How do transactional and transformational leaders influence and lead organisational innovation and change in a telecom company in Sweden?

## Objectives

The dissertation aims to:

1. Critically review transactional and transformational leadership and how each supports innovation and change.
2. Collect semi-structured interview evidence from leaders in “Company T.”
3. Analyse the role business leadership plays in organisational innovation and change.
4. Recommend how change and innovation can be led more effectively.

## How the dissertation was built

### Research design

This is an exploratory, cross-sectional, qualitative case study guided by interpretivism and an inductive approach.

- The case is a large Swedish telecommunications company of approximately 112,000 employees.
- Twenty-two leaders were approached and ten male middle managers participated.
- Six were former colleagues of the researcher and four were recruited through LinkedIn.
- One-hour interviews were conducted by telephone or video call, recorded, and transcribed.
- Open-ended questionnaires and the semi-structured interviews captured leadership experience.
- Fiedler’s Least Preferred Co-worker scale and a Leadership Development Framework were used to classify participants: one transactional, three transformational, and six displaying both sets of traits.
- Data was coded into leadership behaviours and skills, then analysed for themes, patterns, repeated words, emotional emphasis, missing information, and differences from the literature.

### Key literature and concepts

The literature review focuses on:

- **Transactional leadership:** contingent reward, active and passive management by exception, control, task clarity, and short-term exchange.
- **Transformational leadership:** idealised influence, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, and individualised consideration.
- **Change and innovation:** innovation as a multi-stage transformation of ideas; incremental and radical innovation; product, process, market, and organisational forms.
- **Vision and momentum:** leadership vision, Collins’ flywheel and hedgehog concepts.
- **Change management:** Kotter’s eight-step model, coalitions, empowerment, communication, and sustaining change.
- **Trust, values, and empowerment:** long-term commitment and the willingness to experiment.
- **Adaptive leadership and teamwork:** flexibility, decentralisation, and shared problem solving.
- **Learning organisations:** personal development, learning from failure, and organisational capability.
- **Organisational systems:** balancing strategy, structure, people, culture, technology, economic value, and capability.

## Main findings

- All participants were positive about change, but transformational leaders described a more personal commitment to it and more frequently led by example.
- Transformational leaders placed greater emphasis on two-way communication, trust, empowerment, motivation, care, and personal development.
- Leaders with stronger transactional traits focused more on direction, detailed control, tasks, objectives, and pushing changes through.
- Support from top management was critical. Hierarchy, internal politics, inconsistent objectives, and a fear-based climate constrained leaders who wanted to behave more transformationally.
- Innovation required encouragement, psychological safety, simple structures, decentralised decisions, and tolerance of failure. Participants discussed change much more extensively than innovation, suggesting that Company T’s innovation ambition was not fully embedded.
- Transformational participants more closely followed the spirit of Kotter’s model: creating a vision, building coalitions, empowering people, networking, and stabilising changes.
- Transactionally oriented leaders tended to underemphasise empowerment and vision.
- Hard skills—strategy, structure, political judgement, planning, and measurement—remained necessary. The argument is not that “soft” skills replace them, but that hard skills alone are insufficient.
- Leaders with weaker people-oriented skills found it harder to balance organisational subsystems and economic value with long-term capability.

## Conclusion and recommendations

The study concludes that transformational leadership is better suited to innovation and change in a complex, knowledge-intensive organisation because it develops trust, motivation, capability, and commitment. However, most participants displayed a mix of styles, so the evidence does not support a clean binary distinction.

The author recommends:

- develop people-management, cultural-intelligence, and emotional-intelligence skills alongside strategic and political skills;
- strengthen trust, empowerment, decentralisation, and learning from failure;
- ensure senior leadership actively supports innovation and change;
- align structures and incentives with the behaviours the organisation says it values;
- build leadership development around both personal growth and organisational systems;
- study top-management support and gender differences with larger samples.

## Limitations and cautions

- Only ten leaders participated, all male.
- Six participants knew the researcher, who had previously worked for the company.
- Interviews relied on memory, self-description, and English used as an additional language.
- Leadership-style classification was simplified, and six of ten participants displayed mixed traits.
- No employee or objective performance evidence was collected.
- The single-case, interpretive design cannot establish that transformational leadership caused better outcomes.

## Why read this dissertation

Read this for a straightforward example of connecting Bass-style leadership theory to change and innovation practice. It is strongest on interview themes around trust, empowerment, top-management support, and the tension between organisational systems and a leader’s preferred style.
