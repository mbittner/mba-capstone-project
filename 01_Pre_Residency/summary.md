# Pre-Residency Summary

## Overview

This folder contains three documents covering two related areas:

1. **Preparing for the RKC Online Residency** — technical setup, participation etiquette, and introductions to several tutors.
2. **Understanding the MBA capstone dissertation** — assessment structure, research expectations, marking criteria, academic standards, and recommended resources.

> **Important:** The tutor introduction deck is dated February 2022, and the dissertation handbook is for the 2021/22 academic year. Use these documents as background guidance, but confirm current dates, tutors, contact details, assessment requirements, ethics procedures, and policies in the RKC Online Campus and current module materials.

## Source Documents

### `RKC_OnlineResidency_QuickStart_Guide.pdf`

Practical guidance for participating in live Online Residency sessions through Zoom.

Key points:

- Install the **Zoom desktop application** before the first session; the mobile app is an alternative.
- Use a computer or device with a reliable internet connection, microphone, and webcam.
- **Headphones are highly recommended** to improve audio and reduce echo or feedback.
- A higher-quality microphone is optional; an integrated laptop webcam is normally sufficient.
- Use the clock in the **RKC Online Campus** to synchronize with **Zürich time** and avoid timezone errors.
- Join each live session **15 minutes early** to resolve technical problems.
- Select **Connect using computer audio** or **Join with Computer Audio** when entering Zoom.
- Test the speaker and microphone before the session.
- Keep the microphone muted when not speaking. The guide suggests holding the space bar to temporarily unmute.
- The guide was prepared by Dr Radu Negoescu (`radu.negoescu@rkc.edu`).

### `MBK7011M_Dissertation_Handbook_2021-22.docx`

The handbook describes the 60-credit Master's-level capstone dissertation module. Its purpose is to support an independent research project that builds on the MBA programme and uses qualitative, quantitative, or mixed research methods.

Expected learning outcomes include the ability to:

- Define a focused research area, question, and objectives or hypotheses.
- Select and critically evaluate suitable research methodologies.
- Critically engage with relevant contemporary theory, frameworks, and models.
- Conduct independent research.
- Present well-supported findings, conclusions, and recommendations in an appropriate academic format.

## Dissertation Assessment

### Research proposal — 2,000 words, 20% of the module

Indicative structure and weighting:

| Component | Approximate length | Weight |
|---|---:|---:|
| Problem definition; objectives, questions, or hypothesis | 300 words | 15% |
| Literature review | 1,200 words | 60% |
| Methodology and research design | 400 words | 20% |
| Limitations and potential bias | 100 words | 5% |

The proposal should also contain a realistic research timeline. The literature review is the most heavily weighted element and should critically analyze a selective but sufficiently broad and deep body of literature rather than merely summarize it. The methodology should justify the research design, data sources, collection tools, sample, and intended analysis.

Depending on the project, the proposal may require approval from the University's Research Ethics Committee. This must be discussed with the assigned tutor before collecting data.

### Dissertation — 12,000 words, 80% of the module

Indicative marking allocation:

| Component | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Abstract and introduction | 10% |
| Literature review and methodology | 40% |
| Findings, analysis, conclusions, and recommendations | 40% |
| Reflection on the dissertation process | 10% |

Strong work should:

- State a clear, focused research question and objectives.
- Connect the literature review directly to the project design and analysis.
- Explain and justify the research philosophy, approach, strategy, sampling, data-collection instrument, and analysis method.
- Present findings clearly and analyze them against published research.
- Ensure conclusions and recommendations follow logically from the evidence and research purpose.
- Make recommendations that are realistic in the organizational context.
- Critically reflect on the research process, skills used, and personal development needs.

## Academic and Research Requirements

- Use the **Harvard referencing system** specified by York St John University.
- Anonymize organizations and private individuals unless explicit permission has been obtained to use their real names.
- Follow current ethics requirements and obtain any necessary approval **before** beginning primary data collection.
- The handbook expects at least:
  - Two substantive conversations with the tutor before submitting the proposal, including feedback on a draft proposal.
  - Two further conversations about research progress or a draft before submitting the dissertation.
- Proofreading help may address spelling, grammar, and punctuation only. The intellectual content, edits, and corrections must remain the student's own work.
- Academic misconduct, including plagiarism and cheating, is treated seriously. Consult the current University policy.
- Master's-level work is assessed on subject knowledge, critical and independent thinking, communication, and accurate academic conventions. Higher grades require sustained critical analysis, depth and breadth of research, originality, persuasive conclusions, and professional presentation.

## Recommended Next Steps

### Before the Online Residency

- [ ] Confirm the Residency schedule and convert all sessions using the Zürich clock in the RKC Online Campus.
- [ ] Install or update the Zoom desktop app.
- [ ] Test the internet connection, webcam, microphone, headphones, and Zoom computer audio.
- [ ] Choose a quiet workspace with stable connectivity.
- [ ] Plan to join every live session 15 minutes early.
- [ ] Review the current tutor list and note the appropriate contact route for technical or academic issues.

### To Prepare for the Dissertation

- [ ] Check the current module handbook, syllabus, assessment briefs, deadlines, marking rubric, word-count rules, and referencing guide.
- [ ] Identify a feasible business or management problem that can support a focused research question.
- [ ] Perform an initial literature search through the University library to confirm that enough credible and current research exists.
- [ ] Decide provisionally whether the project is qualitative, quantitative, or mixed-methods.
- [ ] Assess access to participants, organizations, and data before committing to the topic.
- [ ] Discuss the topic, feasibility, methodology, confidentiality, and likely ethics requirements with the assigned tutor.
- [ ] Build a research schedule that includes proposal drafting, ethics approval, instrument design and pilot testing, data collection, analysis, dissertation drafting, tutor feedback, proofreading, and submission.
- [ ] Keep written records of tutor conversations and decisions.

## Risks and Items to Confirm

- The documents may not reflect the current academic year.
- No current Residency dates or dissertation deadlines appear in this folder.
- The detailed module schedule is located in the Online Campus syllabus and forum, not in the handbook.
- Ethics approval can affect the research timeline; data collection should not begin until the required approval is confirmed.
- Participant or organizational access is a major feasibility risk and should be validated early.
- The proposal's literature review carries 60% of the proposal marks, so topic selection and literature searching should begin well before drafting.
