# 02_Key_Documents — Summary

## Scope

This summary covers only the files stored directly in `02_Key_Documents`. The contents of the `Great_dissertations`, `Great_proposals`, and `Research_ethics_pack` subfolders were not inspected. `Research_ethics_pack.zip` was reviewed as a top-level file.

## Executive summary

The folder provides four complementary resources for planning and delivering the MBA dissertation:

1. A short development form for defining a feasible topic, access to data, relevant literature, and an initial research question and objectives.
2. A curated set of research-methods readings covering research design, qualitative and quantitative data collection, sampling, analysis, and case studies.
3. A six-page overview of the dissertation process, assessment, timing, supervision, ethics, and success factors.
4. A research ethics pack containing the approval forms and participant-facing templates needed before primary data collection.

The immediate priorities are to narrow the topic, confirm access to suitable evidence or participants, draft a research question and realistic objectives, select and justify a methodology, and establish whether the proposed study qualifies for the University's block/low-risk ethics route. No participant data should be collected until formal ethics approval has been received.

## File-by-file summary

### `Dissertation_development_form_2021.docx`

A one-page planning worksheet intended to support a workshop discussion. It asks for notes on:

- the broad area of interest and specific issues;
- who or what can be accessed, what information can be gathered, and how;
- relevant literature; and
- a draft research question and objectives.

The form is exploratory: ideas recorded on it are not binding and can be changed later.

### `MBK7011M_Dissertation_resources_references.docx`

A reading list for the MBK7011M Capstone Project/Dissertation, last updated in August 2021. It contains 20 authenticated Content Store links that require a York St John University username and password.

The readings cover:

- **Choosing and designing the project:** topic selection, project design, research philosophy, and case-study design.
- **Qualitative data collection:** interviews, focus groups, observation, and general primary-data methods.
- **Quantitative data collection:** questionnaires, survey planning, and general data collection.
- **Sampling:** sampling strategies for applied research.
- **Qualitative analysis:** coding, pattern identification, interpretation, and management of qualitative data.
- **Quantitative analysis:** quantitative analysis, statistical significance, and chi-square testing.
- **General methods:** concise overviews of research methods and applied business-research practice.

This is a useful starting library, but it should be supplemented with recent, topic-specific peer-reviewed literature and current methods sources.

### `The_dissertation_2021.pdf`

A November 2021 overview of the MBA/MA dissertation process. It is guidance rather than a substitute for the applicable Dissertation Module Handbook.

#### Research focus

- The topic must relate to the degree. For the MBA/MA Leading Innovation and Change, suggested broad areas include leadership, innovation, change, strategy, and culture.
- A narrow, clearly defined, feasible focus is preferred to an overly broad study.
- Research may use a deductive approach, testing how theory applies in a setting, or an inductive approach, analysing a setting and then relating the findings to published theory.
- Qualitative, quantitative, or mixed methods may be used, but the choice must be justified and evaluated realistically.
- Topic selection should balance personal interest, programme requirements, access, available time and resources, project complexity, and the researcher's skills.

#### Assessment described in the 2021 document

| Submission | Indicative length | Module weighting |
|---|---:|---:|
| Research proposal | 2,000 words, ±10% | 20% |
| Dissertation | 12,000 words, with the applicable permitted margin | 80% |

The proposal is expected to state the research question and objectives, review relevant literature, and outline the proposed methodology. The dissertation is expected to include the research background, literature review, methodology, findings, analysis linked to literature, conclusions, and recommendations.

The document states that:

- an overall mark of at least 50% is required to pass;
- each submission must receive at least 20%;
- a failed proposal or dissertation may be retaken once;
- material from the proposal may be reused directly in the dissertation because both are part of the same module; normal quotation/self-referencing rules apply to material reused from other assignments; and
- if circumstances force a major change after the proposal, the original proposal mark is retained and a replacement proposal is not normally assessed.

These rules and word limits should be verified against the handbook for the actual enrolment cohort.

#### Indicative timeline described in the 2021 document

- Enrolment can be requested after the Residency.
- The pre-module topic/method questionnaire is due at least two weeks before the module starts so RKC can allocate a suitable supervisor.
- The proposal deadline is described as six weeks after the module begins.
- The dissertation deadline is described as 30 weeks after the module begins.
- Proposal marking is described as taking about four weeks; dissertation work should continue while the proposal is being marked.
- Dissertation marking and moderation may take up to eight weeks.

Actual dates are assigned at enrolment and take precedence over these indicative timings.

#### Supervision and working practices

- Agree communication methods, frequency, draft-feedback expectations, and turnaround time with the supervisor at the outset.
- Use the RKC Thesis Repository for submitting drafts and receiving formal feedback; keep a record of any other communications.
- The supervisor advises, questions, supports, and comments on work, but the student remains responsible for decisions.
- If a supervision issue cannot be resolved directly, contact RKC Student Care.

#### Success factors

- Maintain a clear research question and realistic objectives throughout.
- Ensure every chapter contributes to answering the question and objectives.
- Use current, relevant academic literature and connect the methodology to research-methods literature.
- Plan interviews, focus groups, or surveys early.
- Draft the literature review and methodology before data analysis where practical.
- Allow time to revise the complete dissertation for coherence.
- Seek supervisor feedback early enough for it to be useful.
- Continue working during assessment periods and make use of peer support and the Dissertation Forum.

### `Research_ethics_pack.zip`

The ZIP contains nine Word documents:

1. An introductory guide to the ethics process.
2. A summary of research ethics issues and the criteria for block approval.
3. The contents of the 2022 short/low-risk ethics form.
4. The 2022 full ethics application form.
5. A participant information sheet template for interviews, focus groups, observation, and similar methods.
6. A completed interview information-sheet example.
7. A participant information sheet template for questionnaires.
8. A research consent form template for interviews.
9. A gatekeeper information sheet template.

#### Approval route

- **Block/low-risk route:** complete the electronic short form on the RKC Thesis Repository. It is reviewed by the supervisor and the RKC Research Ethics Panel.
- **Higher-risk route:** notify the supervisor and complete the full YSJU application. Further review may be required from the RKC panel, the Module Director, and/or a York St John University research ethics committee.
- **Non-negotiable dependency:** ethics approval must be received before collecting information from participants.
- A revised ethics submission may be necessary if the data-collection plan or participant population changes.

#### Main conditions for the block/low-risk route

The project must not involve:

- primary material that may be offensive or illegal to access or possess, including material concerning state security, pornography, abuse, illegal behaviour, or terrorism;
- terrorist or extremist groups;
- sensitive subjects such as political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, health, sex life, sexual orientation, or criminal convictions/offences;
- children, vulnerable adults, or people unable to give informed consent without special approval; or
- risks to participant welfare.

The researcher must:

- recruit adults aged 18 or over who can freely give informed consent;
- explain the study, what participation involves, voluntariness, and withdrawal rights;
- anonymise or pseudonymise data and remove identifying contextual details;
- keep identity/code keys separate from the research data;
- store data securely in password-protected locations;
- restrict access to authorised members of the research/supervisory team;
- not share raw, pre-anonymised data;
- comply with GDPR and applicable University policies; and
- show draft recruitment documents and data-collection instruments to the supervisor before use.

The full form also examines overseas research, gatekeepers, covert methods, NHS involvement, incentives, vulnerable participants, researcher safety, intrusive or stressful procedures, data-management flows, special-category data, Data Protection Impact Assessment requirements, data sharing, retention/destruction, and privacy-risk controls.

#### Participant-facing templates

- Information sheets must be adapted to the study and audience, cleared of all instructional/red placeholder text, and shown to the supervisor before being issued.
- Interview/focus-group information sheets should explain the purpose, why the person was invited, what is required, expected timing, confidentiality, data security, voluntariness, withdrawal, and contact details.
- The template gives participants a withdrawal window of up to 14 days after participation; confirm that this remains the approved wording for the current project.
- Interview consent should normally be documented by a signed form or an explicit email. For online questionnaires, proceeding past the consent information/tick box can indicate consent.
- The questionnaire information sheet should state the expected completion time, closing date, anonymity, data protection, and researcher contact details.
- If a gatekeeper helps recruit participants, provide a tailored gatekeeper information sheet and obtain written confirmation of their agreement.
- The consent template includes optional audio recording, transcription, restricted access, and destruction of recordings after the research; retain these clauses only if they accurately describe the approved study.

## Recommended next steps

### Before module enrolment or project confirmation

1. Complete the development form with one or two candidate topics.
2. For each candidate, test feasibility: access to participants/data, sample size, timing, organisational permission, risks, and available literature.
3. Draft one focused research question and three to five measurable, realistic research objectives.
4. Run an initial literature search using current peer-reviewed sources, using the methods reading list as supporting methodological guidance.
5. Choose a provisional qualitative, quantitative, or mixed-method approach and write a short justification linking it to the question and objectives.
6. Select an enrolment/start date that leaves realistic periods for ethics approval, recruitment, data collection, analysis, writing, supervisor review, and revision.

### At the start of the module

1. Confirm the official handbook, assessment brief, word limits, marking criteria, submission dates, and current ethics forms for the enrolled cohort.
2. Submit the pre-module topic/method questionnaire by the notified deadline.
3. Agree a supervision protocol and an initial milestone plan.
4. Determine the ethics route with the supervisor.
5. Draft the relevant participant information sheet, consent wording, gatekeeper material, interview guide, and/or questionnaire.
6. Submit the ethics application and supporting documents early.
7. Do not recruit participants or collect participant data until written approval is received.

### During the research and writing

1. Maintain an evidence trail of ethics approval, consent, anonymisation, data storage, changes to the protocol, and supervisor feedback.
2. Write the literature review and methodology while recruitment/fieldwork is being prepared.
3. Keep the research question, objectives, literature, methods, findings, analysis, conclusions, and recommendations visibly aligned.
4. If the topic, participants, or data-collection method changes, consult the supervisor before proceeding and determine whether renewed ethics approval is required.
5. Allow time for a full-document coherence review rather than treating chapters as independent assignments.

## Important cautions

- The principal guidance PDF and development form are dated 2021, while the ethics forms are dated 2022. They remain useful planning resources but may not reflect the current rules, systems, contacts, templates, or deadlines.
- The current Dissertation Module Handbook, the live RKC Thesis Repository, the applicable YSJU policies, and direct instructions from the supervisor/module team should be treated as authoritative.
- The methods reading list is also dated 2021. The links may still be useful, but a dissertation literature review must include recent and topic-specific research.
- The ethics pack contains both preview/reference forms and templates. The short form explicitly says it should be completed electronically through the Thesis Repository rather than by editing the reference copy.
