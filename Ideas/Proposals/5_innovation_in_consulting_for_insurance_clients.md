# Dissertation Proposal: Innovation in Consulting for Insurance Clients

## Title
**How Consulting Firms Can Better Lead Innovation in Insurance Clients**

---

## 1. Research Background and Context

### 1.1 The Problem in Consulting
Consulting firms deliver innovation projects to insurance clients—strategy recommendations, technology implementations, process transformations. Yet many projects deliver outputs (plans, systems) without producing sustained outcomes. The client struggles to maintain momentum after go-live, becomes dependent on the consulting team, or quietly shelves the initiative when budgets tighten.

The fundamental challenge: How can consulting firms create conditions for lasting, client-owned innovation rather than dependent projects? What practices distinguish consulting engagements that produce lasting value from those that deliver and disappear?

This dissertation examines what consulting firms must do differently to lead sustainable innovation in insurance clients.

### 1.2 Why This Topic Is Important
1. **Professional Relevance**: As a consulting director, this directly impacts your effectiveness and client value.
2. **Business Value**: Better consulting delivery improves client outcomes and consulting reputation.
3. **Innovation & Change Themes**: Combines consulting delivery, innovation, change management, and leadership.
4. **Underexplored Topic**: Most literature focuses on what consultants deliver, not how they enable client capability.
5. **Practitioner Contribution**: Study produces immediately actionable insights.

---

## 2. Research Question and Objectives

### 2.1 Primary Research Question
**What consulting practices, engagement structures, and client-consultant relationship dynamics most effectively enable insurance clients to adopt and sustain innovation beyond the consulting engagement?**

### 2.2 Research Objectives

**Objective 1 (Literature):** To critically review literature on consulting effectiveness, client-consultant relationships, knowledge transfer, change management, implementation, and capability building in professional services contexts.

**Objective 2 (Evidence):** To explore consulting leaders', client sponsors', and delivery team members' experiences during innovation engagements, focusing on what practices enabled or hindered client adoption and sustainability.

**Objective 3 (Analysis):** To identify which consulting practices correlate with sustained client outcomes and what distinguishes successful from unsuccessful engagements.

**Objective 4 (Contribution):** To develop a framework for consulting-led innovation that prioritizes client capability and sustainability.

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Research Design
**Mixed-method case study**: Study one or more consulting-led innovation engagements from proposal through post-implementation, interviewing consulting and client stakeholders and analyzing engagement outcomes.

This approach allows you to:
- Understand consulting practices from both sides of the engagement.
- Trace outcomes backward to identify which practices mattered.
- Capture both process (what consultants did) and outcomes (what stuck).

### 4.2 Data Collection

#### Primary Data: Interviews
- **Sample**: 15-20 interviews including:
  - Consulting engagement leaders/partners
  - Consulting delivery team members
  - Client sponsors/executives
  - Client implementation leads
  - Client end-users or business owners

- **Interview Guide Topics**:
  - Consulting approach and engagement design
  - How client capability was built (or not)
  - How knowledge was transferred
  - Barriers to client ownership
  - Client satisfaction and sustainability post-engagement
  - Lessons learned

#### Secondary Data: Engagement Artifacts
- Consulting proposals and engagement charters
- Delivery plans, governance, and status reports
- Transition/handover plans
- Post-implementation reviews
- Client capability assessments (if available)

#### Tertiary Data: Post-Implementation Outcomes
- System adoption/usage rates
- Business metrics related to innovation
- Whether recommendations were sustained or abandoned
- Client testimony 6-12 months post-engagement

### 4.3 Analysis Approach
- **Consulting Practice Coding**: Identify which consulting practices are used (process consulting, expert-driven, coaching, etc.).
- **Outcome Correlation**: Link practices to client sustainability outcomes.
- **Comparative Analysis**: If multiple engagements, compare what worked across cases.

## 5. Ethical Considerations

**Low-risk route**:
- Standard interviews with consenting professionals.
- No sensitive personal data; consulting engagement is fair topic.
- Client organization may require confidentiality agreements.

**Key safeguards**:
- Anonymize client and participant names.
- Secure data storage; GDPR compliance.
- Discuss confidentiality boundaries with consulting firm and client.
- Do not disclose proprietary consulting methodologies or strategies without consent.
