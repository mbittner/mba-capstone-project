# Friday — Summary

## Overview

Friday consolidates the residency and applies the week's learning to an individual project idea. The folder contains two Word documents:

- an answer key to Thursday's dissertation self-assessment; and
- a preview of the RKC proposal questionnaire used when applying to enrol on the capstone module.

The self-assessment contains both historical module rules and informed good-practice recommendations. Current module documentation must take precedence.

## Main content

### Historical dissertation rules in the answer key

The review states:

| Item | Answer in the supplied material |
|---|---|
| Module duration | 30 weeks from formal module start |
| Proposal period | First 6 weeks |
| Proposal length | 2,000 words plus 10%, maximum 2,200 |
| Dissertation length | 12,000 words plus 10%, maximum 13,200 |
| Proposal weighting | 20% |
| Dissertation weighting | 80% |
| Overall pass | Combined mark of 50% or more |

The stated typical dissertation structure is:

1. Introduction
2. Literature Review
3. Methodology
4. Findings
5. Analysis/Discussion
6. Conclusions and Recommendations
7. Reflection on the Dissertation Process

The abstract and table of contents precede the numbered chapters.

The material states that proposal content may be reused directly in the dissertation without separate acknowledgement because it is part of the same module. Content from unrelated previous assignments remains subject to normal quotation, citation, and academic-integrity rules.

All of these rules should be verified against the current Module Handbook.

### Research question and objectives

The recommended structure is:

- one research question;
- no separate research-aim layer; and
- three or four objectives.

The first objective should normally address the literature to be critically reviewed. Later objectives should specify evidence collection, analysis, and—where justified—conclusions or recommendations.

### Research methods

The answer key confirms that:

- the chosen method's advantages and limitations must be explained using methods literature;
- interviews and questionnaires do not both have to be used;
- neither is mandatory, because focus groups, observation, or document analysis may be suitable;
- eight interviews is presented as a tutor's recommended minimum for an interview-only study, not an immutable rule;
- recording interviews is strongly recommended where feasible and consented to, but is not stated as compulsory;
- full transcription and inclusion of transcripts in the dissertation are not automatic requirements;
- quotations should be used to evidence qualitative findings;
- approximately 75 questionnaire responses is presented as a useful guideline, subject to population size and study design;
- Qualtrics is recommended but another suitable tool may be used; and
- Excel is often sufficient for straightforward analysis, while more complex work may require specialist software and suitably qualified supervision.

Survey invitations must substantially exceed the desired number of usable responses because response rates are uncertain. The material does not prescribe a fixed multiplier.

### Supervisor interaction

The historical handbook is described as requiring at least two interactions during the proposal period and at least two during the remaining dissertation period. The guidance recommends more purposeful contact than the minimum.

Useful feedback points include:

- question and objectives;
- proposal plan or draft;
- interview guide or questionnaire;
- analytical framework or initial coding/statistical approach;
- interpretation of results;
- chapter drafts; and
- a sufficiently early near-final review.

The number, format, turnaround time, and final date for receiving feedback should be agreed directly with the supervisor. Do not leave major draft review close to submission.

### Research ethics

The answer key is unequivocal: do not collect information from respondents before ethics approval.

Ethics principles can also apply to participant information previously collected by an organisation, such as surveys or monitoring data. Existing data is not automatically exempt. Approval, ownership, confidentiality, consent, and lawful use must be considered before incorporating it into the dissertation.

### RKC proposal questionnaire

The questionnaire is completed when applying to enrol on the Capstone Project Module. RKC uses it to identify a supervisor with an appropriate fit and sends it to the prospective supervisor.

The form asks for:

- name and student number;
- completion date;
- proposed title;
- principal aim of the research;
- key terms;
- country or geographical area;
- sector, industry, or company;
- essential sources;
- research method; and
- any additional relevant information.

Although the questionnaire uses “principal aim,” the residency's recommended academic structure uses a research question and objectives. A sensible approach is to express the principal aim as a concise plain-language version of the research question.

## Friday discussion preparation

Based on Wednesday's session instructions, the project discussion is intended to use one provisional idea. The student should be ready to explain it in no more than 10 minutes, leaving approximately 10 minutes for tutor and peer feedback.

A useful presentation sequence is:

1. Research context and problem.
2. Draft research question.
3. Three or four objectives.
4. Essential literature or theoretical lens.
5. Evidence and participants/documents that are realistically accessible.
6. Proposed strategy and method.
7. Sample and recruitment route.
8. Analysis plan.
9. Main ethics considerations.
10. Biggest design uncertainty on which feedback is needed.

The discussion is developmental, not an assessed defence, and the topic or design can change afterward.

## Recommended next steps

### Immediately after the workshop

1. Record the feedback received and separate factual corrections, risks, suggestions, and open choices.
2. Revise the question and objectives for focus and alignment.
3. Reassess access, sample, evidence quality, workload, and ethics.
4. Decide which feedback to adopt and document the reasons.
5. Conduct a targeted literature search to test the revised concept.

### Before completing the proposal questionnaire

1. Confirm current capstone eligibility, dates, form fields, and submission process.
2. Write a concise working title that identifies the phenomenon and context.
3. Express the principal aim/research question in one clear sentence.
4. Select key terms that will help RKC identify the subject and methodological expertise required.
5. Identify several essential, credible academic sources rather than relying on general textbooks.
6. Describe the method precisely—for example, “qualitative case study using 10–12 semi-structured interviews and reflexive thematic analysis,” rather than simply “qualitative.”
7. Use the additional-information field for access constraints, specialist methods, language/geography, sensitive ethics issues, or supervisor expertise needed.

### Once a supervisor is allocated

1. Confirm the question, objectives, strategy, scope, and ethics route.
2. Agree communication and feedback arrangements.
3. Build a complete milestone plan from the official deadlines.
4. Prepare the proposal, research instruments, and ethics documents in parallel where permitted.
5. Do not recruit participants or use participant-derived organisational data until approval is confirmed.

## Important cautions

- The answer key reflects historical module guidance. Word limits, pass rules, chapter structure, contact expectations, and timing may have changed.
- Sample-size numbers are contextual recommendations, not substitutes for methodological justification.
- “Minimum interactions” should not be treated as a target; contact should be planned around meaningful decisions and drafts.
- Existing organisational data may still require ethics approval and owner permission.
- The current online proposal questionnaire may differ from the preview in this folder.
