# Dissertation Proposal: AI Adoption in Insurance

## Title
**How Leadership, Trust, and Governance Shape AI Adoption in Insurance Organizations**

---

## 1. Research Background and Context

### 1.1 The Problem in Insurance
Insurance organizations are investing in artificial intelligence to improve operations, decision-making, and customer experience. But adoption is uneven. Many firms can launch pilots; far fewer can embed AI into everyday practice at scale.

The challenge is not only technical. AI adoption depends on leadership support, employee trust, governance, risk appetite, communication, and the organization’s ability to change how work gets done.

This dissertation explores why AI adoption succeeds in some insurance contexts and stalls in others.

### 1.2 Why This Topic Is Important
1. **Current Business Relevance**: AI is moving quickly into insurance operations and strategy.
2. **Adoption Focus**: The real issue is not just AI availability, but whether people actually use it.
3. **MBA Fit**: Strong links to leadership, innovation, change, governance, and strategy.
4. **Practical Value**: Useful for insurers and consulting firms supporting AI adoption.
5. **Broader Insight**: Findings should transfer to other regulated industries facing similar challenges.

---

## 2. Research Question and Objectives

### 2.1 Primary Research Question
**How do leadership, trust, and governance influence successful AI adoption in insurance organizations?**

### 2.2 Research Objectives

**Objective 1 (Literature):** To review literature on AI adoption, innovation diffusion, technology acceptance, trust in automation, and change management.

**Objective 2 (Evidence):** To explore how insurance leaders and employees experience AI adoption in practice, including what helps and what slows it down.

**Objective 3 (Analysis):** To identify the organizational conditions that enable AI to move from pilot activity to routine use.

**Objective 4 (Contribution):** To develop a practical framework for improving AI adoption in regulated organizations.

## 3. Indicative Literature Review Scope

### 3.1 Core Literature Areas

#### a) Technology Adoption and Acceptance
- Davis, F. D. (1989). Perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, and user acceptance of information technology.
- Venkatesh, V., et al. (2003). User acceptance of information technology: Toward a unified view.

#### b) Trust in Automation and AI
- Research on trust, explainability, transparency, and user confidence in AI-supported decisions.

#### c) Innovation Diffusion and Change
- Rogers, E. M. (2003). *Diffusion of innovations*.
- Literature on change readiness, adoption barriers, and implementation at scale.

#### d) Governance and Responsible AI
- Governance structures, risk management, model oversight, and ethical use of AI in regulated environments.

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Research Design
**Qualitative case study of AI adoption in insurance**: study one or two organizations, or one adoption programme, to understand what shapes adoption success.

### 4.2 Data Collection

#### Primary Data: Interviews
- **Sample**: 12-18 interviews with:
  - business leaders
  - technology and data leaders
  - transformation/change leaders
  - frontline users or business teams
  - governance or risk stakeholders

- **Interview Topics**:
  - why the AI initiative started
  - how adoption was introduced
  - what helped or hindered acceptance
  - how trust in AI was built
  - governance and decision-making
  - how leaders measured value and use

#### Secondary Data: Documents
- business cases
- adoption plans
- training or communication materials
- governance or risk documents
- project updates or lessons learned

### 4.3 Analysis Approach
- Thematic analysis of interview data
- Cross-case or within-case comparison
- Mapping of adoption barriers, enablers, and leadership practices

---

## 5. Ethical Considerations

This should normally be a low-risk study if it focuses on professional experiences and organizational practices.

**Key safeguards:**
- anonymize participants and organizations
- avoid asking for confidential model details or proprietary data
- secure storage and GDPR compliance
- clarify voluntariness and informed consent
