# Dissertation Proposal: Culture as an Enabler of Innovation

## Title
**The Role of Organizational Culture in Enabling Innovation in Insurance Firms**

---

## 1. Research Background and Context

### 1.1 The Problem in Insurance
Insurance organizations frequently espouse innovation as a strategic priority but struggle to translate commitment into consistent innovation behavior. A common issue: organizational culture rewards caution, hierarchy, and risk avoidance—values that protect core business but inhibit experimentation and new ideas.

The cultural paradox: Organizations say "we want innovation" but their systems, norms, and leadership reinforce "we play it safe." This mismatch explains why some insurers run innovation labs that remain disconnected from the core business, or why good ideas from employees never progress beyond suggestion.

This dissertation examines how organizational culture either enables or constrains innovation and what leaders can do to shift it.

### 1.2 Why This Topic Is Important
1. **Culture Is Foundational**: Innovation climate depends on culture; fixing process alone won't unlock innovation if culture remains risk-averse.
2. **Insurance-Specific Challenge**: Regulation and risk management make caution deeply embedded in insurance culture.
3. **Leadership Lever**: Culture change is a leadership responsibility; understanding how to influence it is strategically valuable.
4. **Sustainable Advantage**: Strong innovation culture creates lasting competitive advantage beyond individual projects.
5. **MBA Alignment**: Core theme of the program—culture, leadership, and innovation intersection.

---

## 2. Research Question and Objectives

### 2.1 Primary Research Question
**What organizational cultural attributes most strongly enable innovation in insurance firms, and what leadership practices can shift culture toward greater innovation orientation?**

### 2.2 Research Objectives

**Objective 1 (Literature):** To critically review literature on organizational culture, innovation climate, cultural change, and the relationship between culture and innovation outcomes, with attention to regulated and financial services contexts.

**Objective 2 (Evidence):** To explore how culture is experienced and described by leaders and employees in insurance organizations, including shared norms, assumptions, and behaviors that support or inhibit innovation.

**Objective 3 (Analysis):** To identify which cultural attributes correlate with higher innovation adoption and what leadership actions demonstrably shift culture.

**Objective 4 (Contribution):** To develop a culture assessment framework and actionable recommendations for leaders seeking to build innovation-enabling culture in insurance.

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Research Design
**Qualitative ethnographic or longitudinal case study**: Interviews, observation, and cultural artifacts analysis to understand culture deeply.

This approach allows you to:
- Observe norms and behaviors, not just ask about them.
- Understand culture at multiple organizational levels.
- Track culture change over time if feasible.

### 4.2 Data Collection

#### Primary Data: Interviews
- **Sample**: 15-20 semi-structured interviews across organizational levels:
  - Executive/cultural leaders
  - Middle managers
  - Individual contributors
  - Innovation team members
  - Business-as-usual teams

- **Interview Guide Topics**:
  - Shared norms and "how we do things here"
  - Stories about innovation success and failure
  - Risk tolerance and fear of failure
  - Hierarchical vs. empowered decision-making
  - How ideas are valued or dismissed
  - Leadership role in cultural messaging

#### Secondary Data: Cultural Artifacts
- Innovation policy and strategy documents
- Organizational communications, newsletters, announcements
- Innovation award criteria and nominations
- Risk and compliance policies (reveal what is truly valued)
- Physical workspace design
- Meeting norms and decision-making processes

#### Tertiary Data: Observation
- If access permits, observe meetings, innovation forums, or strategy sessions to see culture in action.

### 4.3 Analysis Approach
- **Coding**: Identify cultural themes (values, norms, assumptions) using Schein's framework (artifacts, espoused values, underlying assumptions).
- **Pattern Analysis**: Map which cultural elements correlate with innovation behaviors.
- **Narrative Analysis**: Analyze stories and language to understand cultural meaning-making.

## 5. Ethical Considerations

**Low-risk ethics route**:
- Standard interviews with consenting professionals.
- No sensitive personal data; organizational culture is fair game for inquiry.
- Observation (if conducted) is of professional meetings, not intrusive.

**Key safeguards**:
- Anonymize participants and organization details.
- Secure data storage.
- Transparency about what is being observed or asked.
- Gatekeeper approval from organization.
