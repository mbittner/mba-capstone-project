# Dissertation Proposal: Psychological Safety and Innovation

## Title
**The Impact of Psychological Safety on Employee-Driven Innovation in Insurance Organizations**

---

## 1. Research Background and Context

### 1.1 The Problem in Insurance
Innovation depends on employees sharing ideas, challenging the status quo, and experimenting. Yet insurance culture often makes people risk-averse about speaking up: the industry penalizes mistakes, values caution, and has hierarchical decision-making. Employees with good ideas may stay silent rather than risk criticism or failure.

Psychological safety—the belief that you can take interpersonal risks without negative consequences—is foundational to innovation climate. Without it, ideas stay unshared and improvement remains limited to formal initiatives.

This dissertation examines the link between psychological safety and innovation behavior in insurance and what leaders can do to build safety.

### 1.2 Why This Topic Is Important
1. **Research-Backed Concept**: Psychological safety has strong academic support (Amy Edmondson's work).
2. **Leadership Practice**: Leaders directly influence psychological safety through behavior and norms they set.
3. **Innovation Impact**: Direct link to innovation outcomes through speaking up and experimentation.
4. **Measurable**: Can be assessed through surveys and correlated with innovation metrics.
5. **Actionable**: Leaders can improve safety through specific, observable behaviors.

---

## 2. Research Question and Objectives

### 2.1 Primary Research Question
**How does psychological safety influence employee-driven innovation behavior in insurance organizations, and what specific leadership actions most effectively build psychological safety for innovation?**

### 2.2 Research Objectives

**Objective 1 (Literature):** To critically review literature on psychological safety, team climate, innovation behavior, and leadership's role in creating safety, with attention to regulated and risk-averse organizational contexts.

**Objective 2 (Evidence):** To explore the relationship between psychological safety (as experienced by employees) and innovation behavior (idea sharing, experimentation, speaking up).

**Objective 3 (Analysis):** To identify which leadership behaviors most effectively build psychological safety and how safety translates to observable innovation outcomes.

**Objective 4 (Contribution):** To develop practical guidance for insurance leaders on building psychological safety for innovation.

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Research Design
**Mixed-method case study**: Combine qualitative interviews with a climate/safety survey to understand how psychological safety and innovation behavior are linked in insurance.

This approach allows you to:
- Measure safety and correlate with innovation outcomes.
- Understand mechanisms (what leadership actions create safety).
- Capture employee experience and voice.

### 4.2 Data Collection

#### Primary Data: Interviews (Qualitative)
- **Sample**: 15-18 interviews across different teams/organizations including:
  - Team leaders/managers
  - Individual contributors
  - People who have spoken up (and what happened)
  - People who haven't (and why)

- **Interview Guide Topics**:
  - What makes it feel safe (or unsafe) to speak up
  - Leader behaviors that increase or decrease safety
  - Examples of ideas shared, experimented with, shelved
  - Relationship between feeling safe and innovation participation

#### Secondary Data: Survey (Quantitative)
- Administer Edmondson's Psychological Safety Scale to 50-100 employees if access permits.
- Correlate safety scores with:
  - Innovation participation (idea submission, experiments)
  - Adoption of new practices
  - Satisfaction and engagement

#### Tertiary Data: Observation and Documents
- Observe team meetings or innovation forums to see if people speak up.
- Review innovation submissions or idea-management data to correlate with team safety scores.

### 4.3 Analysis Approach
- **Correlation Analysis**: Link psychological safety scores to innovation behavior.
- **Thematic Analysis**: Extract themes about leadership behaviors that create safety.
- **Mechanism Mapping**: Understand how safety translates to innovation outcomes.

---

## 5. Ethical Considerations

**Low-risk route**:
- Interviews about team climate and safety (standard workplace topics).
- Optional survey using Edmondson's validated scale.
- No sensitive personal data.

**Key safeguards**:
- Anonymize participants and teams.
- Assure confidentiality of survey responses.
- Avoid identifying individuals who did or didn't speak up.
- Secure data storage; GDPR compliance.
