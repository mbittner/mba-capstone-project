# Dissertation Proposal: Leadership Styles and Change Adoption

## Title
**Which Leadership Styles Are Most Effective in Driving Change in Insurance Organizations?**

---

## 1. Research Background and Context

### 1.1 The Problem in Insurance
Change initiatives in insurance frequently encounter resistance and stall despite strong business cases. A common denominator in failures: the leader driving the change did not inspire commitment or engage stakeholders effectively. Insurance culture amplifies this challenge—employees are cautious, compliance-minded, and skeptical of dramatic change.

The question: Which leadership approaches actually work best when leading change in insurance? Is it transformational leadership (inspiring a compelling vision), servant leadership (supporting people through change), adaptive leadership (responding to resistance), or some combination?

This dissertation examines which leadership styles most consistently produce positive change adoption and business outcomes in insurance.

### 1.2 Why This Topic Is Important
1. **Leadership Module Focus**: Essence of leadership is a core MBA theme; this is a direct application.
2. **Measurable Outcomes**: Leadership style can be assessed and correlated with adoption/satisfaction metrics.
3. **Practical Utility**: Leaders want to know "what actually works"—this study answers that question.
4. **Insurance-Specific Insight**: Leadership effectiveness may differ in regulated, risk-averse culture.
5. **Consulting Value**: Identifying effective leadership styles informs leader coaching and development.

---

## 2. Research Question and Objectives

### 2.1 Primary Research Question
**Which leadership styles are most effective at driving sustained change adoption in insurance organizations, and how do contextual factors (e.g., change scale, organizational maturity, risk profile) influence the effectiveness of different styles?**

### 2.2 Research Objectives

**Objective 1 (Literature):** To critically review literature on transformational, servant, adaptive, and inclusive leadership styles, their impact on change adoption, and findings specific to regulated or risk-averse organizational contexts.

**Objective 2 (Evidence):** To explore leaders' and employees' experiences during change initiatives, focusing on observed leadership behaviors, their perceived effectiveness, and adoption outcomes.

**Objective 3 (Analysis):** To identify which leadership styles (or combinations thereof) most consistently correlate with positive adoption and change success in insurance.

**Objective 4 (Contribution):** To develop a practical guide for leaders on which styles to adopt in different change contexts in insurance.

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Research Design
**Comparative case study**: Study 2-3 change initiatives or programs with different leadership approaches to identify which styles correlate with better outcomes.

This design allows you to:
- Compare how different leaders approached the same type of change.
- Link leadership behaviors to observable adoption outcomes.
- Control for some contextual variables by studying similar programs.

### 4.2 Data Collection

#### Primary Data: Interviews
- **Sample**: 20-25 interviews including:
  - Change sponsors and program leaders
  - Middle managers implementing change
  - Employees/end users experiencing change
  - Change impact assessors

- **Interview Guide Topics**:
  - Leadership behaviors observed (how did the leader communicate, engage, decide?)
  - How well the leader inspired commitment
  - How well the leader handled resistance
  - Perceived leadership effectiveness
  - Impact on adoption rates and job satisfaction

#### Secondary Data: Adoption Metrics
- System usage rates
- Training completion rates
- Change satisfaction surveys
- Business metrics (productivity, quality)

#### Tertiary Data: Document Review
- Communications issued by leaders
- Meeting notes/governance records
- Post-implementation reviews

### 4.3 Analysis Approach
- **Behavioral Coding**: Identify which leadership behaviors were demonstrated (transformational, servant, adaptive, etc.).
- **Correlation Analysis**: Link behaviors to adoption metrics and satisfaction ratings.
- **Thematic Analysis**: Extract themes about what made leadership effective or ineffective.
