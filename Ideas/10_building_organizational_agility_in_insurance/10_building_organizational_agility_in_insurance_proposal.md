# Dissertation Proposal: Building Organizational Agility in Insurance

## Title
**Leadership Strategies for Building Organizational Agility in Legacy Insurance Systems**

---

## 1. Research Background and Context

### 1.1 The Problem in Insurance
Insurance organizations must respond quickly to market changes, regulatory shifts, and competitive threats, yet legacy structures, complex governance, and hierarchical decision-making slow everything down. Even organizations that adopt "agile" delivery methods at the team level find the organization remains slow due to bureaucracy, siloed decision-making, and risk-averse governance.

The challenge: How can insurance leaders build organizational agility (not just team agility) without losing the governance and control essential for a regulated business?

This dissertation examines what organizational conditions enable agility in legacy, regulated environments and what leadership actions drive those conditions.

### 1.2 Why This Topic Is Important
1. **Current Industry Need**: Agility is a top priority in insurance; it's a real, urgent problem.
2. **Innovation & Change Theme**: Central to both innovation and leadership modules.
3. **Organizational Design Angle**: Moves beyond delivery methodology to organization-wide effectiveness.
4. **Consulting Application**: Helps consultants advise on organizational redesign for agility.
5. **Leadership Challenge**: Requires leaders to balance competing values (speed vs. control, empowerment vs. governance).

---

## 2. Research Question and Objectives

### 2.1 Primary Research Question
**What organizational structures, governance approaches, and leadership practices enable insurance firms to build organizational agility while maintaining regulatory compliance and risk management?**

### 2.2 Research Objectives

**Objective 1 (Literature):** To critically review literature on organizational agility, organizational design, governance, decision-making speed, empowerment, and how agility operates in regulated industries.

**Objective 2 (Evidence):** To explore how leaders in insurance are attempting to build agility, what structures they've changed, and what impact they've seen on decision speed and organizational responsiveness.

**Objective 3 (Analysis):** To identify which organizational and leadership practices most effectively improve responsiveness while preserving necessary control and compliance.

**Objective 4 (Contribution):** To develop a practical framework for building agility in regulated, legacy-heavy organizations.

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Research Design
**Case study of organizational agility initiatives**: Study one or two legacy insurance organizations' attempts to build agility, examining structural changes, governance shifts, and leadership approaches.

This allows you to:
- Understand organizational redesign in practice.
- Link structure/governance changes to agility outcomes.
- Identify tradeoffs between agility and control.

### 4.2 Data Collection

#### Primary Data: Interviews
- **Sample**: 16-20 interviews including:
  - Organizational design or transformation leaders
  - Business unit heads
  - Product or delivery leads
  - Compliance or risk leads
  - Employees at various levels experiencing agility changes

- **Interview Guide Topics**:
  - What organizational structures or governance were changed
  - How decision-making has changed (faster/slower, different people)
  - How empowerment was increased or limited
  - What tension exists between agility and compliance
  - How leaders support agility while managing risk
  - Observed changes in responsiveness and outcomes

#### Secondary Data: Organizational Documents
- Organizational charts (past and current)
- Governance frameworks and decision policies
- Job descriptions and authority levels
- Process maps and decision timelines
- Post-change assessments or lessons learned

#### Tertiary Data: Observation
- Observe decision-making meetings or processes if access permits.
- Note how hierarchical or collaborative decisions actually are.

### 4.3 Analysis Approach
- **Organizational Mapping**: Compare structural and governance changes.
- **Decision Speed Analysis**: Track how long decisions now take vs. previously.
- **Empowerment Assessment**: Assess changes in who makes decisions and how.
- **Agility-Control Tradeoff**: Analyze how organizations balance competing values.

## 5. Ethical Considerations

**Low-risk route**:
- Interviews about organizational structure and decision-making.
- Standard consent and confidentiality.
- No sensitive personal data.

**Key safeguards**:
- Anonymize organization and participants.
- Avoid proprietary strategic or governance details.
- Secure data storage; GDPR compliance.
- Discuss with HR or organizational contact about sensitive areas.
