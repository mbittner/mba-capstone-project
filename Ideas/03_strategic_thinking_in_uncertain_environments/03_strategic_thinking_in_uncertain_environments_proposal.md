# Dissertation Proposal: Strategic Thinking in Uncertain Environments

## Title
**Strategic Thinking for Innovation Under Uncertainty in the Insurance Industry**

---

## 1. Research Background and Context

### 1.1 The Problem in Insurance
Insurance leaders face persistent uncertainty: regulatory changes, emerging technology disruption (AI, blockchain, digital competitors), shifting customer expectations, and legacy system constraints create an environment where strategy must evolve constantly. Yet many insurers struggle with strategic decision-making about innovation investment, often oscillating between overcommitment to unproven technologies and paralysis from risk.

The challenge: How do leaders think strategically and make good innovation decisions when the future is fundamentally uncertain? And how do they balance exploration (long-term innovation) with exploitation (short-term operational stability)?

This dissertation examines the strategic thinking practices and frameworks that enable insurance leaders to make better innovation decisions under uncertainty.

### 1.2 Why This Topic Is Important
1. **Core MBA Theme**: Strategic thinking is central to the Leading Innovation and Change curriculum.
2. **Real-World Relevance**: Insurance industry uncertainty is genuine and growing; this topic speaks to actual leadership challenges.
3. **Practical Frameworks**: The study can produce usable strategic thinking tools or decision models.
4. **Consulting Application**: Understanding strategy-making helps consultants advise clients more effectively.
5. **Career Relevance**: As a director, your strategic decision-making directly affects outcomes.

---

## 2. Research Question and Objectives

### 2.1 Primary Research Question
**What strategic thinking practices and frameworks do insurance leaders use to make effective innovation investment decisions under uncertainty, and which practices most consistently improve decision quality?**

### 2.2 Research Objectives

**Objective 1 (Literature):** To critically review literature on strategic thinking, decision-making under uncertainty, scenario planning, innovation strategy, and strategic foresight, with attention to regulated industries and insurance contexts.

**Objective 2 (Evidence):** To explore how senior leaders in insurance actually approach strategic decisions about innovation, including what frameworks they use (or don't use), how they involve others, and how they manage uncertainty and competing priorities.

**Objective 3 (Analysis):** To identify which strategic practices correlate with better decision outcomes and how uncertainty is managed in successful versus unsuccessful innovation strategies.

**Objective 4 (Contribution):** To develop a practical strategic thinking framework or decision model for innovation investment in insurance.

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Research Design
**Qualitative case study with focus on decision processes**: Examine one or more strategic decisions (e.g., technology investment, innovation portfolio choices) from formulation through outcomes.

This approach allows you to:
- Observe strategic thinking in action, not in hindsight.
- Understand the frameworks and mental models leaders actually use.
- Trace how uncertainty was managed and decisions evolved.

### 4.2 Data Collection

#### Primary Data: Interviews
- **Sample**: 12-18 interviews with:
  - Strategy executives or chief innovation officers
  - Divisional leaders making innovation investment decisions
  - Portfolio managers
  - Risk/compliance leads (who constrain strategy)
  - Consultants advising on strategy

- **Interview Guide Topics**:
  - How strategic decisions are framed
  - What frameworks or models inform decisions
  - How uncertainty is assessed
  - How competing priorities are balanced
  - How stakeholders are engaged in strategy
  - Post-decision outcomes and learning

#### Secondary Data: Strategic Documents
- Strategy papers, business cases, innovation roadmaps
- Board or governance meeting minutes
- Risk assessments and scenario analyses
- Post-implementation reviews
- Strategic planning materials

#### Tertiary Data: Observation (Optional)
- Attend strategic planning sessions or portfolio review meetings if access permits.

### 4.3 Analysis Approach
- **Coding**: Identify strategic thinking practices, decision frameworks, and uncertainty management approaches.
- **Pattern Analysis**: Map which practices appear in successful vs. less successful decisions.
- **Process Mapping**: Trace how strategic decisions actually unfold, not how policy says they should.
